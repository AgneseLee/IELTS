# Commercialising Cultural Traditions for Tourism

## 题目

Some people think that cultural traditions will be destroyed when they are used as money-making machines aimed at tourists. Other people believe that this is the only way to save such traditions in the world today. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

## 共用逻辑提纲

- 立场：更认同“适度商业化能够保护传统”，但旅游开发必须受到文化保护政策约束；它并非字面意义上的唯一办法，而是在当今社会中一种现实且重要的手段。
- 主体段 1：迎合游客的商业开发会给传统文化带来压力 → 表演和产品偏离原本含义，并忽视少数群体的声音 → 地方特色和文化认同被削弱。
- 主体段 2：受监管的文化旅游带来收入和公众关注 → 资金支持文化遗产保护，接触传统的游客和居民增加 → 地方经济得到发展，公众对本国历史的认识提高。
- 判断：第二种观点更有说服力，因为传统若缺乏持续的资源和社会关注，也可能逐渐消失；但经济目标不能凌驾于文化真实性和当地群体之上。
- 结论：商业化本身既非必然破坏，也非自动拯救传统；政府的文化保护政策决定旅游收入能否转化为长期保护。

## 模板直替版（299 words，Estimated Band 6.5）

The debate surrounding **the commercial use of cultural traditions for tourism** has engaged a broad audience recently. While some people argue that **turning traditions into tourist products will destroy them**, others believe that **earning money from tourists is the only practical way to preserve them today**. From my perspective, I am more convinced by **the latter**, as it rests not only on more ample theory but also on stronger evidence.

It is important to recognize that **the commercial use of cultural traditions** can produce harmful effects on **traditional culture**. A central problem is that it increases **pressure to tailor ceremonies, performances and cultural products to tourist demand**, which places additional pressure on **local communities**. As a consequence, this approach often generates new problems such as **the loss of original meaning, minority voices, local uniqueness and cultural identity**, instead of delivering the intended benefits. This clearly shows why its negative impact should not be underestimated.

Another notable advantage of **regulated cultural tourism** is its ability to directly optimize the current problem. The main reason is that it provides immediate and targeted improvements in **tourism income, funding for heritage protection and public awareness of national history**, allowing **local communities that maintain cultural sites and activities** to see visible progress without delay. As a result, this approach not only stabilizes the situation in the short term but also creates favorable conditions for further long-term solutions.

In conclusion, although both perspectives have their own merits, I am more convinced by **the view that regulated commercial use can preserve cultural traditions**, because **the claim that all tourist-oriented commercial activity destroys culture** fails to fully address **the need for funding, public awareness and local economic support**. By contrast, **regulated cultural tourism** not only avoids this shortcoming but also offers a more comprehensive and convincing foundation.

## 模板最小修改版（319 words，Target Band 7.0–7.5）

The debate surrounding **the commercial use of cultural traditions for tourism** has engaged a broad audience recently. While some people argue that **turning traditions into tourist products will destroy them**, others believe that **earning money from tourists is the only practical way to preserve them today**. From my perspective, I am more convinced by **the latter, provided that effective cultural policies regulate commercialization**.

It is important to recognize that **uncontrolled commercialization** can produce harmful effects on **traditional culture**. A central problem is that it increases **pressure to satisfy tourist expectations**, which places additional pressure on **local communities to modify ceremonies, performances and cultural products**. As a consequence, this approach often generates new problems such as **the loss of original meaning, minority voices and local uniqueness**, instead of delivering the intended benefits. **When commercial appeal becomes the main standard, the version presented to visitors may no longer represent the community that created it.** This clearly shows why its negative impact should not be underestimated.

Another notable advantage of **regulated cultural tourism** is its ability to **provide practical support for preservation**. The main reason is that it provides immediate and targeted improvements in **funding for heritage protection**, allowing **local communities to maintain cultural sites and activities while benefiting from tourism**. As a result, this approach not only **supports the local economy** in the short term but also **increases awareness of national history and creates favourable conditions for long-term preservation**. **Government policies can ensure tourism revenue serves these aims while protecting cultural meaning.**

In conclusion, although both perspectives have their own merits, I am more convinced by **carefully regulated commercialization**, because **complete rejection of tourist income** fails to fully address **the need for continuing resources and public awareness**. By contrast, **regulated cultural tourism** not only avoids this shortcoming but also offers a more comprehensive and convincing foundation. **It is therefore an important means of saving traditions, although not literally the only one.**

## 自然版（320 words，Target Band 7.0–7.5）

People disagree over whether presenting cultural traditions to paying tourists destroys them or offers the best chance of keeping them alive. Although commercial pressure can weaken the meaning and uniqueness of a tradition, I believe that carefully regulated cultural tourism is an important, though not exclusive, means of preservation because it supplies both resources and public attention.

Those who oppose commercialization are understandably concerned that tourist demand may begin to determine how a culture is represented. Local communities can come under pressure to alter ceremonies, performances or cultural products so that they are easier to sell. Once commercial appeal becomes the main standard, the version shown to visitors may lose its original meaning or fail to include minority voices. This process can weaken both local uniqueness and residents’ sense of cultural identity. From this perspective, treating a tradition as a source of profit may preserve its outward appearance while damaging what it actually represents.

However, cultural tourism can also give vulnerable traditions practical support. Visitors bring income into local economies, and some of this money can be directed towards maintaining heritage sites and sustaining cultural activities. Tourism also exposes both residents and outsiders to national history, thereby increasing public awareness of why particular customs deserve protection. Without continuing resources or social interest, traditions may decline even if they remain commercially untouched. Cultural policies are therefore crucial: governments should ensure that tourism revenue supports heritage protection while local communities retain control over the meaning and presentation of their culture.

In conclusion, commercial use does not inevitably destroy traditions, just as it does not automatically save them. I find the second view more persuasive because regulated tourism can combine economic support with cultural preservation and wider historical awareness. Nevertheless, financial goals must remain subordinate to cultural authenticity and the voices of the communities concerned; under these conditions, tourism is a realistic way, rather than the only possible way, to keep traditions alive.
