## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a new law you would like to introduce in your country**

> **You should say:**
> - What law it is
> - What changes this law has
> - Whether this new law will be popular
> - How you came up with the new law
> - And explain how you feel about this new law

> Bank: B8 | Modules: Semporna / daily-habits / clean-up

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: The law targets plastic waste in places where it can enter the sea quickly and gives visitors and businesses practical alternatives.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: A ban on single-use plastic in protected coastal areas.
- Background: Seeing a sea turtle while freediving near Semporna made marine pollution feel personal.
- Main event: Coastal businesses would offer reusable alternatives, while tour operators would bring their waste back to shore.
- Ability shown: I turned a broad environmental concern into a focused law with practical steps.
- Reflection: I strongly support the law, provided small businesses receive affordable alternatives during the transition.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The law I'd like to introduce is <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">a ban on single-use plastic</mark> in protected coastal areas. It would cover beaches, marine parks, and tourist islands. <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Shops</mark> could no longer <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">give out</mark> plastic bags, cups, or straws. Instead, they would offer reusable options. Tour operators would bring waste back to shore. I came up with the idea after freediving near Semporna with Stella. We saw a sea turtle, and our guide explained how plastic can harm marine life. I think most <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">visitors</mark> would support the law, although some <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">businesses</mark> might initially <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">object</mark>.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
That trip made plastic pollution feel personal. Since then, Stella and I have carried reusable bottles and refused unnecessary bags. Those habits help, but they cannot solve marine pollution alone. A clear national law would make <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">responsible</mark> choices easier for <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">everyone</mark>. It could also create a visible <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">community</mark> result instead of relying on careful <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">tourists</mark>.

The change should be fair. The <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">government</mark> could give small businesses time and help them find affordable alternatives. To me, protecting the ocean is <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">a shared responsibility</mark>. This law would not solve <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">every environmental</mark> problem, but it could still make a real difference. I would feel <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">proud</mark> and hopeful if it were introduced.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
