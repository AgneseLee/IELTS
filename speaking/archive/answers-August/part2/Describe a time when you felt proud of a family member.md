## Cue Card

**Describe a time when you felt proud of a family member**

> **You should say:**
> - When it happened
> - Who the person is
> - What the person did
> - And explain why you felt proud of him/her

> Bank: B8 | Modules: mother / community-accounts / quiet-contribution

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: My mother used her accounting experience to organise confused records and protect a community programme.
- Secondary Ability: Leadership & Collaboration
- Reason: She supported other volunteers and created a system they could continue using without her.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: I felt proud when my retired mother helped a community group.
- Background: Its confused accounts threatened activities for older residents.
- Main event: She checked receipts and built a clear, reusable spreadsheet without payment.
- Ability shown: Practical problem-solving and quiet collaboration.
- Reflection: Her professional skill produced a visible community result after retirement.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
I felt proud of my mother last year when she volunteered for a community group in Zhanjiang. She is a retired accountant, and the group had confused financial records that made it difficult to fund weekly activities for older residents. She spent several evenings checking receipts, correcting mistakes, and creating a clear spreadsheet. She accepted no payment and explained the new system patiently to another volunteer. Later, an older resident thanked her because the clearer accounts helped preserve a weekly meal programme. My mother mentioned this only casually during a phone call.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I felt deeply proud because she made a meaningful practical difference without seeking recognition. Her problem-solving skills turned years of professional experience into something useful for people around her. She also showed collaboration by making the system simple enough for others to maintain.

The event changed my view of achievement outside paid work. I once connected success mainly with promotions or awards. My mother's quiet contribution showed me that reliability matters when a community depends on it. I later helped turn the spreadsheet into a reusable template. Looking back, I admire both what she achieved and how she did it: calmly and without seeking attention. Practical skills can keep creating value long after a formal career ends.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
