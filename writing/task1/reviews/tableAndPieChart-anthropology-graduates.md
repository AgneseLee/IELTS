## Final Integrated Version(168) (7.0–7.5)

The pie chart shows the destinations of anthropology graduates from one university, while the table compares salaries in three employment sectors after five years.

Overall, full-time work was by far the most common destination, whereas part-time work combined with postgraduate study was the least common. Most people in every employment category earned at least $75,000, and government employees were most likely to earn $100,000 or more.

Regarding destinations, 52% of graduates entered full-time work, over three times the proportion taking part-time jobs (15%). Another 12% were unemployed. Full-time postgraduate study and unknown destinations each accounted for 8%, while only 5% combined part-time employment with further study.

Turning to salaries, 40% of freelance consultants occupied each of the top two brackets. Government employees were more concentrated at the upper end: 30% earned $75,000–$99,999 and 50% received at least $100,000. For both groups, only 5% earned $25,000–$49,999 and 15% made $50,000–$74,999. Private-company employees showed a different distribution, with 10%, 35%, 25% and 30% in the four ascending salary bands, respectively.
