## Cue Card

**Describe a person who loves to grow plants (e.g. vegetables, flowers) at home or in the garden**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - What plants he/she grows
> - How he/she grows the plants
> - And explain why he/she loves growing plants

> Bank: B8 | Modules: balcony-garden / rain-damage / daily-routine

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: My mother changed the plants' location and watering schedule after heavy rain damaged them.
- Secondary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: She improves her gardening through observation, regular care, and experience.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: My mother, a retired accountant who grows vegetables on her balcony in Zhanjiang.
- Background: She grows spring onions, lettuce, and tomatoes in recycled containers.
- Main event: After heavy rain damaged the tomatoes, she moved them indoors and changed her watering routine.
- Ability shown: Careful observation, continuous learning, and adaptability.
- Reflection: Gardening gives her calm, visible progress and a meaningful daily routine.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to describe is my mother, a retired accountant in Zhanjiang. She grows spring onions, lettuce, and tomatoes in recycled containers on her balcony. She chooses the seeds, mixes the soil herself, and waters the plants regularly without using many chemicals. Last summer, heavy rain damaged several tomato plants. She moved them indoors, removed the weakest leaves, and adjusted the watering schedule. Most of them recovered, and we later ate the tomatoes during a family meal.

What stands out most is her patience and careful observation. She never expects immediate results.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
Instead, she watches small changes and learns from experience. The rain also showed her adaptability because she changed her approach instead of abandoning the plants.

More importantly, gardening gives her a peaceful daily routine after retirement. She enjoys producing healthy food, but the deeper value is seeing steady progress from simple actions. Watching her has changed my view of learning. Improvement does not always require formal lessons or expensive equipment. It can come from attention, practice, and a willingness to respond when conditions change. I have now started growing herbs beside my window. Her example reminds me that small habits can make a meaningful practical difference over time.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
