## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a person you know who has a successful business**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - How you got to know him/her
> - Why and how he/she started the business
> - What business he/she does
> - And explain why you think the business is successful

> Bank: B8 | Modules: consultancy / client-trust / responsible-growth

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: Chen explains technical choices honestly and protects client trust when a project becomes difficult.
- Secondary Ability: Leadership & Collaboration
- Reason: He coordinates a small team responsibly and avoids growth that would create unmanageable workloads.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Chen, my former manager and founder of a small software consultancy.
- Background: I met him at my previous company, where he noticed smaller firms needed affordable digital tools.
- Main event: He started with three colleagues and built the business through honest advice, reliable service, and word of mouth.
- Ability shown: Clear client communication and responsible leadership.
- Reflection: I define his success through loyal clients, stable work, and sustainable growth rather than size alone.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to describe is Chen, my former manager. I got to know him at my previous software company, where he often solved practical client problems. He noticed that many small businesses needed simple digital tools but could not afford a large technology firm. He therefore started a software consultancy with three colleagues. They build small business systems and provide reliable after-sales support. I once saw Chen recommend a cheaper solution because it suited a client's needs better. That client later returned and introduced two other companies through word of mouth.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I consider the business successful because it creates value rather than chasing quick profit. It has loyal clients and manageable growth.

Chen's communication skills are central. He explains choices plainly and discusses delays early, which helps him build trust when a project is difficult. He also shows leadership by protecting his team's workload and taking responsibility for mistakes. Watching him changed my idea of success. A company does not need to expand rapidly or attract constant attention. It can make a meaningful practical difference through reliable work and long-term relationships. If I ran a business, I would want the same balance between profit, honest service, and a healthy team. That is more convincing than size alone.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
