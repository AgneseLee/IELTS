## Cue Card

**Describe one of your friends who learned something without a teacher**

> **You should say:**
> - Who he/she is
> - What he/she learned
> - Why he/she learned this
> - And explain whether it would be easier to learn from a teacher

> Bank: B1 | Modules: podcasts / language-exchange / Malaysia-directions

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: Stella builds language practice into daily life, records expressions in context, and improves through regular use without formal lessons.
- Secondary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: She uses English in real situations, listens actively, and focuses on making herself understood.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my former colleague and close friend, who taught herself English and conversational Japanese.
- Background: She wanted to communicate confidently while travelling and with international colleagues.
- Main event: She shadows podcasts daily, attends a weekly language exchange, and used English when we were lost in Malaysia.
- Ability shown: Consistent independent learning supported by practical communication.
- Reflection: A teacher could correct mistakes faster, but her self-directed routine is more flexible and sustainable.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The friend I'd like to describe is Stella, whom I met about three years ago on the same software team. She taught herself English and can now <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">hold conversations in Japanese</mark> as well. She wanted to communicate confidently while travelling and working with international colleagues. Instead of taking lessons, she shadows short podcasts every morning, records <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">new expressions</mark> in complete sentences, and joins a weekly language exchange. During our Malaysia trip, our navigation app failed, so she used English to ask other tourists for directions. She didn't worry about making <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">mistakes</mark>, and we soon found our way.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I think learning with a teacher might be easier. A teacher could correct her pronunciation immediately and organise the material. However, fixed lessons wouldn't suit her busy schedule.

What impressed me most was her <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">discipline</mark> and relaxed <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">attitude</mark> towards mistakes. She listens actively, adjusts her wording, and cares more about meaning than <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">perfect</mark> grammar. Seeing her progress changed how I study English. I used to collect advanced vocabulary but hesitate when speaking. Now I practise familiar phrases aloud and focus on making myself understood. Stella reminds me that <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">confidence</mark> grows through repeated use. For her, self-study may be slower in some areas, but it is flexible, practical, and easy to maintain.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
