## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a person who met difficulties but succeeded**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - What difficulties he met
> - How he overcame the difficulties
> - And explain how you feel about him

> Bank: B4 | Modules: layoff / work-routine / healthier-boundaries

## 1. Band 7 Answer

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
About three months ago, my company went through a layoff. And my friend Stella, also one of my colleagues, suddenly received a much heavier workload. Then she started feeling overwhelmed and burned out. She said she also couldn't fall asleep at night because she was worried about what might happen if she couldn't handle all these tasks or if she messed things up. She was literally concerned about everything. But instead of <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">panicking</mark>, <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">she made a new work routine </mark>and honestly talked to her manager and asked him for help. Luckily, her manager listened to what she was going through and helped her reorganize her workload and priorities. She also stopped responding to non-urgent messages at night and set clear boundaries between work and her life. She also developed a new habit of taking a fifteen-minute walk after work. All these changes helped her step out of her negative feelings, and finally, she ended up sleeping better and didn't have that kind of sleep problem anymore.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I really <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">admire</mark> her because she showed me what real strength is. Her success didn't happen overnight. She adjusted because she realized her old habits weren't suitable anymore. So she tried to improve her situation by breaking challenges into smaller, more manageable steps she could handle. That's why I admire her. She's a really charming and capable person.

Looking back, her experience also changed my view of resilience. I used to think strong people just kept working even under huge pressure. But now I think strength also means asking for help, accepting support, and changing direction when things don't go as planned. And that's why her experience still stays with me. It reminds me to slow down, adjust, and move forward steadily.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->

## 2. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: Stella changed her work routine and boundaries when her old habits no longer helped her cope.
- Secondary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: She asked her manager to help reorganize her workload and divided challenges into manageable steps.

## 3. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my friend and colleague.
- Background: After a layoff at our company, her heavier workload left her overwhelmed, burned out, and unable to sleep.
- Main event: She asked her manager for help, stopped responding to non-urgent messages at night, and began taking a daily walk; she eventually slept better.
- Ability shown: Changing an unhealthy routine, accepting support, and breaking challenges into manageable steps.
- Reflection: I admire her because her recovery showed me that strength includes asking for help and changing direction.
