## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a tall building you like or dislike**

> **You should say:**
> - What it is used for
> - Where it is
> - What it looks like
> - And explain why you like/dislike it

> Bank: B7 | Modules: office-skyscraper / city-view / daily-comfort

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Creativity & Innovation
- Reason: I looked beyond the skyscraper's appearance and came up with practical design ideas for ventilation, lifts, and shared outdoor space.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: An office skyscraper near my former workplace in Beijing.
- Background: Stella and I visited it several times for meetings.
- Main event: Its glass exterior, bright atrium, and city view impressed me, although crowded lifts and sealed windows reduced daily comfort.
- Ability shown: I explored ways the building's existing design could better serve everyday users.
- Reflection: The experience taught me to judge innovation by how well it solves ordinary problems.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
A tall building I like is an office skyscraper near my former workplace in Beijing. Stella and I visited it for meetings. It has a glass exterior, a bright atrium, and upper floors with a wide city view. The building mainly contains offices, along with a few cafés and shared meeting rooms. I liked the natural light and efficient use of limited urban land. However, the lifts were crowded during rush hour, and the sealed windows sometimes made the offices feel disconnected from fresh air. Overall, I like the building despite these practical weaknesses.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
Visiting it changed the way I think about innovation. A dramatic skyline is not enough if ordinary users face frustration every day. The experience encouraged me to think creatively and explore practical improvements. Better lift management, accessible outdoor areas, and natural ventilation could make the tower healthier and easier to use. This is creativity I value because it improves an existing method instead of chasing novelty. Looking back, the building taught me to balance appearance with function. I now judge architecture by the experience it creates over time, not by one photograph. That makes my opinion more realistic: I admire the tower, but I can still imagine how it could serve people better.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
