## Cue Card

**🧡 Describe someone you know who enjoys learning about history**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - How he/she learns history
> - Why he/she loves history
> - And explain how you feel about him/her

> Bank: B2 | Modules: Stella / history-conversation / perspective-on-worries

> Logic: work anxiety → conversation with Stella → historical perspective → smaller worries → calmness

## 1. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
About three months ago, I had trouble falling asleep. I just couldn't fall asleep naturally, you know. I would lie in bed overthinking my career and my future. I was basically worried about everything that was bothering me. Anyway, that's why I talked to my best friend Stella. She's **the kind of person who enjoys learning about history**. She usually learns about history by reading books and watching documentaries. Last month, I invited her over to my apartment in Beijing for a catch-up. We just lay on the sofa talking about history, literature, movies, and music, not about cars, money, houses, or those boring things, you know. Our conversation eventually drifted into my concerns about work and my future. Stella comforted me by telling me **how small people can seem when we think about the incredibly long span of human history**, you know, hundreds of thousands of years.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">When she said that, I suddenly realized how small my worries seemed in that bigger context. My concerns suddenly seemed quite trivial. Although it didn't fix everything right away, <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">it turned out to be quite helpful. </mark>I learned to look at things from a different angle. Compared with the <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">massive scale of history</mark>, many things I was concerned about seemed small and trivial.
