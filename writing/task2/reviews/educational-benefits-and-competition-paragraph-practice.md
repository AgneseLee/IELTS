## Revision Advice

### Task Achievement

- [TA] 正向段的 `vocational education → practical skills → labour-market demand → higher salaries` 是一条有效且切题的因果链。
- [TA] 从“更多技能型学生进入劳动力市场”直接推到 `social mobility` 存在逻辑跳步。生产率会因技能供给增加而提升，但社会流动还需要说明弱势背景学生如何获得更高收入、向上跨越收入阶层。
- [TA] 负向段准确覆盖了心理压力和家庭经济负担两方面。
- [TA] `educational competition → after-school tutoring` 之间缺少机制。补充家长为防止孩子落后而购买辅导，可以让经济负担的来源更可信。

### Coherence and Cohesion

- [CC] 两段都有清楚的主题句、解释和结果句，正反组织合理。
- [CC] `On the other hand` 通常用于引出对立面；这里的补课费用是同一负面立场下的第二个影响，应使用 `Moreover` 或 `In addition`。
- [CC] `education thus improves` 中 `thus` 与前面的 `In the long run` 并不重复，但因果依据不足；应先补出向上收入流动的机制，再用 `thereby` 引出结果。

### Lexical Resource

- [LR] `individual payment` 不能表示长期收入能力，应改为 `individuals' earning potential`。
- [LR] `vocational education syllabus` 把“职业教育体系”误写成“单门课教学大纲”，宜用 `vocational curricula` 或 `vocational programmes`。
- [LR] `barge in the labour market` 语体不当；`barge in` 表示鲁莽闯入，应写成 `enter the labour market`。
- [LR] `the particular job-related skills` 既不需要定冠词，含义也略显重复，可简化为 `relevant job-specific skills`。
- [LR] `are usually supposed to be expensive` 不表示“通常很贵”，应写为 `is often expensive`。

### Grammatical Range and Accuracy

- [GR] `those who lacks` 应为 `those who lack`，因为 `who` 指代复数 `those`。⚠ recurring：主谓一致仍是反复出现的问题。
- [GR] `recognize educational competition can...` 应补上宾语从句连接词：`recognize that educational competition can...`。
- [GR] `the intense competitions` 应为不可数形式 `intense competition`。
- [GR] `demands, which places` 应为 `demands, which place`；关系代词指代复数 `demands`。⚠ recurring
- [GR] `tutoring, which are` 应为 `tutoring, which is`；`tutoring` 在此不可数。
- [GR] `well-beings` 应为不可数名词 `well-being`；所有格堆叠处用 `the well-being of both students and their parents` 更自然。

## Final Integrated Version(138) (7.0–7.5)

Education can improve both individual prosperity and society's economic performance. Vocational programmes equip students with practical, job-specific skills that employers need, helping graduates secure better-paid work and improve their living standards. When such opportunities are accessible to students from disadvantaged backgrounds, education can also enable upward income mobility. A larger pool of skilled workers may, in turn, raise national productivity.

Nevertheless, intense educational competition can harm the well-being of students and their families. Rising performance expectations add to the pressure on learners who already face heavy workloads, increasing the risk of stress and burnout. Moreover, families may feel compelled to pay for costly after-school tutoring so that their children do not fall behind. This expenditure can place a substantial financial burden on parents, meaning that excessive competition may damage family well-being rather than deliver the intended educational benefits.
