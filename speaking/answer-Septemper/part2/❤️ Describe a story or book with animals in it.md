## 1. Band 7 Answer

About three months ago, I had trouble falling asleep. I just couldn't fall asleep naturally. I would lie in bed worrying about my career and my future. I was basically worried about everything that was bothering me.

One night, I couldn't fall asleep again, so I picked up a book from my shelf which I had bought several years ago but had never read. The book was _Counseling for Toad_. It's a psychology book with animal characters, including Toad, Heron, Rat, Mole and Badger.

It follows the conversations between Heron and Toad and how Heron, as a counselor, helps Toad understand where his negative feelings came from and make sense of his life. I found the book warm, clear and practical. I also recognized myself in Toad, so I was curious about how he responded to those conversations and how he gradually overcame his negative feelings.

What left the strongest impression on me was the idea that **“slow is fast.”** I used to think I had to handle every challenge alone and solve every problem immediately because I thought that would prove I was strong and capable enough. But that put a lot of pressure on me and made me feel exhausted and nervous.

After reading the book, I started writing down my feelings instead of pushing them aside. I realized that slowing down didn't mean falling behind. Instead, it helped me focus on what I could control and what mattered most at the moment. Now, slowing down means taking time to understand what's going wrong before taking action and facing my negative feelings directly instead of ignoring them.

**That change in perspective is why the book has stayed with me.**
