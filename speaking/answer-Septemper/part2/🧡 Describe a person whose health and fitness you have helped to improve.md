## Cue Card

**🧡 Describe a person whose health and fitness you have helped to improve**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person was
> - Why you helped him/her
> - How you helped him/her
> - And explain whether it was easy or difficult

> Bank: B4 | Modules: layoff / advice / manageable-steps

## 1. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
About three months ago, my company went through a layoff, and Stella, one of my best friends and also my colleague, suddenly received a much heavier workload. She started sleeping badly and feeling overwhelmed. When I noticed what she was going through, I just wanted to help her get through it. At first, I didn't want to overwhelm her with a long list of advice. I just listened carefully while she talked about what was stressing her out. Then I suggested she take a short 15-minute walk after work. If I was free, I would join her. I also encouraged her to talk to her manager about those unrealistic deadlines because I thought reducing stress was important for our mental health.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
Honestly, it wasn't easy at the beginning because sometimes she was too tired to do anything, and I could really relate to that. I knew how that felt. Anyway, I told her, “Just break things down.” When a huge problem feels like something we can't handle all at once, we can break it down into smaller, more manageable steps. That helps us focus on one small thing at a time instead of feeling overwhelmed by the whole problem. And luckily, she did. Over time, she became a bit more active and felt less exhausted. That experience also changed me a little bit. Now, when my friends are struggling, I don't jump in with advice or tips right away. I ask them what kind of support they want. I think that simple question helps people feel supported and understood. So yeah, it was challenging at first, but I'm really glad I was able to help her.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->

- Secondary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: I suggested short walks that fitted her limited energy and time.

- Reflection: Useful support starts with what the other person can maintain.
