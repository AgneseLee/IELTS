# 1. Person who overcame difficulties

## Core Skills

Primary:
Adaptability

Secondary:
Problem-solving Skills

## Band 7 Answer

The person I'd like to describe is Stella, a close friend and former colleague of mine. She went through a difficult period after our company laid off several employees. The people who remained had to take on much more work, and Stella gradually became exhausted. She slept badly, doubted herself and still tried to protect the rest of the team.

**Before seeing her deal with burnout, I believed strong people simply worked harder and kept their worries to themselves. Stella initially did that too, but it clearly wasn't sustainable.** The turning point came when she admitted that the workload was affecting her health. She spoke honestly to her manager, listed the deadlines that were impossible to meet and asked the team to agree on priorities.

She also divided her recovery into manageable steps. She stopped replying to non-urgent messages late at night, took short evening walks to clear her head and accepted help from colleagues. Their support made her feel understood and supported. Gradually, her sleep and confidence improved, and she later moved into a healthier role with clearer boundaries.

Watching her changed the way I respond to uncertainty. **I used to feel overwhelmed when several career problems appeared at once and would try to solve everything alone. Now I focus on what I could control, break a large problem into manageable steps and adjust my approach when necessary.** Her experience taught me that resilience is not about hiding pressure. It is about accepting support and looking at setbacks from a different perspective. That lesson will remain valuable whenever I face changes in my career or personal life.

I admire his willingness to challenge an established method and invest in a long-term technical goal, although I do not agree with everything he says or does.

What interests me is the way SpaceX treated rocket reuse as both a creative idea and a practical system. Reaching that goal required engineers from many fields to work together, test designs and learn from mistakes. Musk did not solve every technical issue, but he communicated a clear direction and brought together people who could turn ideas into working results.

**Before watching the video, I associated creativity with individual talent. I imagined that an innovative leader simply produced a brilliant idea and persuaded others to follow it. Afterwards, I understood that innovation also depends on collaboration.** A leader has to explain the purpose, invite specialist knowledge and create a process in which experiments can improve the idea.

This changed how I behave in software projects. **I used to protect an early solution because I wanted it to feel like my own work. Now I divide work into manageable steps, share prototypes sooner and learn from other engineers' feedback.** That produces a stronger result than working alone. Musk's example also reminds me to look at setbacks from a different perspective. Overall, he showed me that imagination becomes useful when a capable team can turn ideas into working results. I hope to use those abilities if I build an AI product in the future.
