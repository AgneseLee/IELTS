## Cue Card

**Describe a time when you worked in a group** ❤️

> **You should say:**
> - What you did
> - Who you worked with
> - What problems you faced
> - And explain why you worked in the group

> Bank: B4 | Modules: unclear-roles / daily-check-in / completed-project

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Leadership & Collaboration
- Reason: I helped four colleagues divide responsibilities and coordinate their work toward a shared deadline.
- Secondary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: Short daily check-ins allowed us to raise problems early and resolve disagreements clearly.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Four colleagues and I built an internal reporting tool.
- Background: The six-week project required different design, coding, and testing skills.
- Main event: We fixed duplicated work by assigning clear roles and holding daily check-ins.
- Ability shown: Team coordination and open communication.
- Reflection: Clear responsibility turned different strengths into a successful result.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
I worked in a group of four colleagues at my former software company. We had six weeks to build an internal reporting tool, so the project needed design, coding, and testing skills from different people. At first, our roles were unclear. Two people repeated the same work while an important testing task was ignored, and tension grew as the deadline approached. I suggested dividing the work according to our strengths and holding a ten-minute check-in each morning. Stella handled design, I built the system, and the others tested it. We finished on schedule.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
This experience strengthened my leadership and collaboration skills because I learned that teamwork needs visible responsibility, not just friendly intentions. The short meetings helped us coordinate without wasting time, and they made problems easier to discuss before frustration became personal.

Admitting our first method had failed was harder than the technical work. Once we focused on the process rather than blame, our abilities became complementary. Our manager later asked us to demonstrate the tool elsewhere, giving us a genuine sense of achievement. Since then, I have reused the check-in method. The project taught me that a strong team communicates early, respects different opinions, and shares responsibility for the result.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
