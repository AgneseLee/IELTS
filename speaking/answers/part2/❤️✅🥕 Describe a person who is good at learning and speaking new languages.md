## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a person who is good at learning and speaking new languages**

> **You should say:**
> - How you got to know him/her
> - How he/she learns a new language
> - What languages he/she can speak
> - And explain how you feel about him/her

> Bank: B1 | Modules: podcasts / language-exchange / Malaysia-directions

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: Stella builds language practice into daily life, records expressions in context, and improves through regular use.
- Secondary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: She prioritises making herself understood, listens actively, and treats mistakes as useful feedback.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my former colleague and friend, who speaks English and conversational Japanese.
- Background: I got to know her about three years ago while working on the same software team.
- Main event: She shadows podcasts daily, attends a weekly language exchange, and used English when we were lost in Malaysia.
- Ability shown: Consistent learning supported by confident, practical communication.
- Reflection: Her method made me value active use more than waiting for perfect grammar.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to describe is Stella, whom I met about three years ago when we worked on the same software team. She speaks English confidently and can also <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">hold conversations in Japanese</mark>. To learn, she listens to short podcasts every morning, shadows the speakers, and records <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">new expressions</mark> in complete sentences. Once a week, she joins an online language exchange. During our Malaysia trip, our navigation app failed, so she used English to ask other tourists for directions. She didn’t worry about making <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">mistakes</mark>. She simply asked for directions, and we soon found our way.

I admire her <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">discipline</mark> and her <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">attitude</mark> towards mistakes. She treats them as feedback instead of evidence that she lacks talent. She cares more about meaning than <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">perfect</mark> grammar.

Her example <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">changed</mark> my English study. I used to collect advanced vocabulary but hesitate when speaking. Now I retrieve familiar phrases and request correction only when a mistake blocks meaning. Practice has become more useful. Stella reminds me that <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">confidence</mark> does not arrive before we speak; it grows through repeated use. Her progress genuinely motivates me.
