## Cue Card

**Describe a person who likes to look after the natural world**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - What he or she does
> - How he or she does it
> - How often he or she does it
> - And explain how you feel about this person

> Bank: B8 | Modules: Semporna / clean-up / daily-habits

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Leadership & Collaboration
- Reason: Stella invites friends to clean-ups, works with volunteers, and encourages change without blaming people.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my friend and former colleague who cares about marine nature.
- Background: Seeing a sea turtle in Semporna made plastic pollution feel personal to her.
- Main event: She uses low-waste habits daily and joins a river clean-up about once a month.
- Ability shown: She cooperates with others and makes environmental action easy to join.
- Reflection: Her quiet consistency has influenced my habits more than dramatic claims would.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to describe is Stella, my friend and former colleague. She became especially protective of nature after we saw a sea turtle while freediving near Semporna. In daily life, she carries a reusable bottle, avoids excessive packaging, and uses public transport when practical. About once a month, she also joins a weekend clean-up near a river or park. She works with other volunteers, collects litter, and sorts recyclable material. She invited me to one of these events, and together we saw a visibly cleaner area by the end of the morning.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I admire her because she turns concern into regular action. She explains why a habit matters without blaming people who are learning.

That approach shows leadership and collaboration. By making activities friendly and practical, she motivates others and creates a visible community result. Her influence changed my routine, so I now carry a reusable bottle and refuse unnecessary bags. More importantly, I realised that protecting nature is a shared responsibility, not something left entirely to governments. One clean-up cannot solve plastic pollution, but repeated habits can make a meaningful practical difference. Stella taught me that consistency is more convincing than a dramatic speech. I respect her because she makes these choices even when nobody is watching.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
