# IELTS Speaking Part 3 困难题短答案

> 仅保留 40 道不容易直接由模块现场展开的问题。每题使用 1–2 个核心模块，答案控制在 3–4 句；其余问题按照 [`part3-question-map.md`](part3-question-map.md) 现场组合。

## 人物与能力

### 01 Are children born smart, or do they learn to become smart?

Both natural ability and learning matter, but education usually has the greater long-term effect. Regular practice and useful feedback help children improve their reasoning and problem-solving skills. Natural talent may give someone a faster start. However, it rarely leads to strong performance without curiosity and sustained effort.

### 02 Why are some people well-rounded while others are good at only one thing?

It often depends on how they use their time. Some people explore different subjects and develop a broad range of skills. Others concentrate on one field because specialist knowledge is valuable in their career. Neither approach is automatically better. A person needs enough general ability for daily life and deeper expertise for demanding work.

### 03 What are the advantages and disadvantages of being a famous child?

Fame can give a child unusual opportunities, such as professional training and financial rewards. However, constant public attention may reduce privacy and create pressure to behave perfectly. Children are still developing emotionally, so criticism can be especially harmful. Their parents should therefore protect their education, rest and right to make ordinary mistakes.

### 04 What can children do to become famous?

They usually need a visible skill, such as music, sport or online content creation. Consistent practice can improve that skill, while media platforms help them reach a wider audience. However, becoming famous also depends on timing and luck. Children should focus on genuine development rather than treating attention as the only sign of success.

### 05 Which works better in environmental protection, rewards or punishment?

I think a combination works best. Rewards make greener choices attractive, while penalties create clear consequences for serious pollution. For example, public transport discounts can encourage change, but companies still need fines for illegal emissions. The policy should make responsible behaviour easier and harmful behaviour more costly.

### 06 Who should people ask for help, colleagues or family members?

It depends on the problem. Colleagues usually understand technical tasks and workplace expectations, so their advice is more practical at work. Family members may provide stronger emotional support and a more independent perspective. The best choice is the person with relevant knowledge who can also be trusted.

### 07 Is it better to focus on a few subjects or learn many subjects?

Students need both breadth and depth. Several subjects give them basic knowledge and help them discover their interests. Later, focusing on fewer areas allows deeper understanding and stronger practical skills. The balance should therefore change with age rather than forcing young children to specialise too early.

### 08 Why do some people grow food instead of buying it?

Home-grown food can feel fresher and gives people greater control over how it is produced. Gardening is also a relaxing form of physical activity. However, it requires time, space and basic skills, so it is not always cheaper or more convenient. Most people will probably combine home growing with ordinary shopping.

### 09 Should children plan their future careers?

They should explore possible careers, but they do not need a fixed plan too early. Research helps them understand the skills and education different jobs require. However, their interests and the labour market may change. A useful plan should provide direction while remaining flexible enough to accommodate new information.

### 10 What makes a business successful?

A successful business normally solves a real customer problem at a reasonable price. It also needs reliable service, careful cost control and the ability to adapt to market changes. Advertising may attract attention, but it cannot compensate for a weak product. Long-term success depends more on customer trust and consistent value.

## 关系、教育与建议

### 11 Will online communication replace face-to-face communication?

I do not think it will replace it completely. Online tools are faster and help people maintain relationships across distance. Face-to-face contact still provides tone, body language and shared experiences, which build deeper trust. The two forms will coexist because they satisfy different communication needs.

### 12 Should galleries and museums be free?

Basic access should be free or affordable because museums provide education and preserve shared culture. This allows low-income families and students to benefit as well. However, exhibitions and historic buildings are expensive to maintain. Public funding, donations and charges for special exhibitions can share the cost without creating a high entry barrier.

### 13 What is the difference between reading a book and visiting a museum?

Books usually provide more detailed explanations and allow readers to learn at their own pace. Museums offer physical objects and a stronger visual experience, so history can feel more immediate. Books are easier to access repeatedly, while museums create memorable direct contact. They complement each other rather than serving the same purpose.

### 14 Why do many children think education is boring?

Some lessons rely too heavily on passive listening and memorisation. Children then struggle to connect abstract information with real life, so their attention falls. Practical tasks, discussion and clear examples can make learning more active. However, students also need to accept that not every useful skill is immediately entertaining.

### 15 Why are young people sometimes unwilling to listen to older people?

Their experiences may come from a different social and technological environment. Young people can therefore feel that the advice is no longer relevant. Older people may also present experience as a rule instead of explaining the reasoning behind it. Respectful discussion works better when both sides acknowledge that circumstances have changed.

### 16 Are modern cities more suitable for young or old people?

Large cities often suit young adults because they provide more jobs, education and entertainment. Older people may find the crowds, cost and fast pace tiring. However, cities also offer specialist healthcare and convenient public transport. Suitability depends less on age itself than on accessibility, housing and personal lifestyle.

### 17 Is a building's exterior or interior more important?

The interior is usually more important because it determines safety, comfort and practical use. An attractive exterior can improve a street and give a building cultural value. However, appearance should not consume money needed for accessibility or maintenance. Good architecture should combine function with a design that suits its surroundings.

### 18 Is it better to live in a new or old building?

New buildings usually have better insulation, modern facilities and lower maintenance needs. Older buildings may offer larger rooms, established communities and historical character. Their actual condition matters more than their age. I would choose a well-maintained older home over a poorly built new one.

## 消费、地点与媒体

### 19 How are street markets different from large shopping malls?

Street markets often offer lower prices, local products and direct contact with sellers. Shopping malls provide greater comfort, consistent quality and more services in one place. Markets can feel more distinctive, while malls are usually more convenient. People's choice depends on whether they value price and atmosphere or reliability and comfort.

### 20 How do shopping habits differ across age groups?

Younger consumers are generally more comfortable with online shopping and digital payment. Older shoppers may prefer physical stores because they can inspect products and ask staff for help. However, the difference is becoming smaller as apps become easier to use. Income and lifestyle can be as important as age.

### 21 Does spending money make people happy?

It can create short-term pleasure, especially when people buy a useful product or share an experience with others. However, repeated impulse buying often provides only temporary satisfaction and may create financial stress. Spending contributes more to well-being when it supports genuine needs, relationships or meaningful activities.

### 22 Why do some people use noise as background sound while working?

Steady background sound can cover sudden distractions and make a space feel less empty. Some people also associate certain music with concentration. However, speech or unpredictable noise can compete for attention and reduce accuracy. The effect depends on the task and the person's working habits.

### 23 What disadvantages do residents face in popular tourist cities?

Large visitor numbers can create crowding, noise and additional waste. Tourism may also increase demand for short-term rentals, pushing up housing costs for local residents. Although it creates jobs and business income, unmanaged tourism can weaken community life. Cities need visitor controls and housing rules to balance these interests.

### 24 Are students overly dependent on AI?

Some students are, especially when they accept generated answers without checking them. This may save time but weakens independent thinking and prevents teachers from seeing genuine ability. AI is still useful for explanations and feedback. Students should use it to support their reasoning, not replace the learning process.

### 25 Why are some people better at persuading others?

They usually understand what the other person values and adjust their explanation accordingly. Clear communication makes the reasons easier to follow, while trust makes the listener more willing to consider them. Confidence helps, but aggressive pressure often has the opposite effect. Effective persuasion combines empathy, evidence and respect.

### 26 Is it good to ask strangers online for advice?

It can be useful because strangers may offer varied experience without emotional bias. However, their identity and expertise are difficult to verify. Online advice is therefore suitable for collecting possibilities, not making high-stakes decisions by itself. People should compare sources and consult a qualified person when the consequences are serious.

### 27 Why do people become angry when messages receive no reply?

Silence creates uncertainty because people cannot tell whether the message was ignored or simply missed. They may interpret the delay as disrespect, especially when the matter feels urgent. However, the other person may be busy or need time to respond. Clear expectations about urgency can prevent unnecessary conflict.

### 28 How can you tell whether someone is a good leader?

A good leader communicates priorities clearly and takes responsibility for decisions. Team members should understand their roles and feel safe raising problems. Results matter, but stable cooperation and staff trust are also important evidence. A leader who succeeds only through constant pressure is unlikely to remain effective.

### 29 What do you think about children working hard only for grades?

Grades can provide a clear goal, but they should not become the only reason to learn. Score-driven study may encourage memorisation and create unnecessary pressure. Children also need curiosity, practical skills and the confidence to make mistakes. Assessment should measure progress while preserving a broader purpose for education.

## 文化、休闲与公共议题

### 30 How is watching sport in a stadium different from watching it on TV?

A stadium provides a stronger atmosphere because spectators share the excitement with a crowd. Television is cheaper and more convenient, and it offers replays and expert commentary. Live attendance creates a memorable social experience, while TV provides better access. The choice depends on cost, distance and the importance of the match.

### 31 Is imagination essential for scientists?

Yes, because scientists need to form new explanations and imagine solutions that do not yet exist. Technical knowledge helps them test those ideas, but knowledge alone may only reproduce established methods. Imagination should therefore work with evidence. Creative ideas become valuable only when they can be examined and verified.

### 32 Why do older and younger people prefer different music?

People often develop strong musical preferences during adolescence, so different generations are attached to different artists and styles. Younger listeners are also exposed to new music through social media. However, streaming platforms now make older music widely available. Personal experience may gradually matter more than age.

### 33 How are urban and rural transport systems different?

Cities usually have denser public transport because large populations create regular demand. Rural areas cover longer distances with fewer passengers, so frequent services are more expensive to operate. This often makes residents more dependent on private vehicles. Flexible buses and shared transport can improve access without copying an urban network.

### 34 Are laws about mobile-phone use necessary?

They are necessary when phone use creates a clear public risk, such as distracted driving or secret recording. Everyday manners should usually be guided by social rules rather than criminal law. Excessive regulation would be difficult to enforce and could limit reasonable personal use. Laws should target specific harm.

### 35 Do people prefer local or national news?

Local news is more relevant to daily life because it covers transport, schools and community events. National news has wider political and economic importance. Many people use both, but their attention changes with circumstances. A local emergency makes community reporting essential, while a major election shifts interest to national coverage.

### 36 Why is national identity important?

A shared identity can create belonging and encourage people to support common institutions. It also helps traditions and historical knowledge pass between generations. However, national identity should not be used to reject other cultures or minority groups. It is healthiest when it is confident, inclusive and open to exchange.

### 37 Should pets be kept in cities?

Yes, if owners can provide enough space, exercise and long-term care. Pets offer companionship and emotional support, especially in smaller households. The main problem is irresponsible ownership rather than urban life itself. Housing rules should protect neighbours while still allowing suitable pets.

### 38 Why do people regard pets as family members?

Daily care creates routines and a strong emotional bond. Pets also provide companionship without the social pressure found in some human relationships. Over time, owners feel responsible for their health and safety. That combination of affection, shared time and responsibility makes the relationship feel like family.

### 39 What is the most important factor in an advertisement?

The message must be clear and relevant to the intended customer. Creative images can attract attention, but people still need to understand the product and its value. Credibility is also essential because exaggerated claims weaken trust. A successful advertisement is memorable without misleading its audience.

### 40 Will there ever be a law that everyone accepts?

Probably not, because people have different values, interests and cultural backgrounds. Even a useful law may impose costs on a particular group. Governments should therefore aim for broad legitimacy rather than complete agreement. Transparent consultation and consistent enforcement can increase acceptance, but universal approval is unrealistic.
