## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a place you have travelled to that you would like to recommend to others**

> **You should say:**
> - What it is
> - Where it is
> - What you saw and did there
> - And explain why you would like to recommend it to others

> Bank: B3 | Modules: Semporna / freediving / sunrise

> Logic: L1-light · emotional reset: stress → pause → steady action → clearer mind

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: I followed safety instructions, controlled my breathing, and adjusted calmly to freediving in an unfamiliar marine environment.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Semporna, a coastal destination in Sabah, Malaysia.
- Background: Stella and I spent the final week of our ten-day Malaysia trip there.
- Main event: We watched the sunrise, tried local food, and went freediving above coral reefs where we saw a sea turtle.
- Ability shown: I adapted to an unfamiliar activity and respected the instructor's safety guidance.
- Reflection: Emotional reset: stress → pause → steady action → clearer mind.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
I'd like to recommend Semporna, a coastal area in Sabah, Malaysia. I took the trip after work pressure had started affecting my sleep. Semporna gave me the distance I needed to reset my routine. Stella and I visited it last year during a ten-day trip and stayed for about a week. We tried local dishes, watched the sunrise, and took a boat to several small islands. The <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">highlight</mark> was freediving for the first time. Under the clear water, I saw colourful coral, schools of fish, and a sea turtle moving below us.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I followed the instructor's safety guidance and kept a respectful distance from the wildlife. The scenery was beautiful, but the underwater experience made the place <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">unforgettable</mark>. That became the turning point I needed. Instead of pushing harder, I paused and focused on one manageable step at a time. Once I slowed down, I could think more clearly and make fewer mistakes. The improvement was gradual, but my sleep and confidence both recovered. I realised that slowing down does not mean falling behind; steady action can move me forward faster. Since then, when pressure builds, I protect my sleep, reduce unnecessary tasks, and notice ordinary moments. I now feel calmer and enjoy daily life more.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
