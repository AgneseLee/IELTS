## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a time when you sent a message or an email to someone but received no reply for a long time**

> **You should say:**
> - Who you sent it to
> - What the message/email was about
> - Whether you finally received the reply
> - And explain how you felt about the experience

> Bank: B4 | Modules: project-message / polite-follow-up / delayed-reply

> Logic: L1-full · emotional recovery: pressure → pause → manageable steps → calmness

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: I explained the deadline clearly and followed up politely without blaming Stella.
- Secondary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: I managed the uncertainty, adjusted my assumptions, and focused on the actions within my control.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: I sent a project message to Stella, my former colleague.
- Background: I needed technical information from her before an approaching deadline.
- Main event: After three days without a reply, I followed up politely and eventually received the information.
- Ability shown: Clear communication, patience, and adaptability under uncertainty.
- Reflection: Emotional recovery: pressure → pause → manageable steps → calmness.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
I remember sending a message to Stella, my former colleague, while we worked on a software project. The silence made me worry, and I slept badly while imagining the worst. I eventually realised that repeated messages would only increase the pressure. I needed technical information to finish my section before a deadline. I sent it on Tuesday but received no reply for three days. I assumed she had <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">overlooked</mark> it and became <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">stressed</mark>. Instead of sending repeated messages, I waited one day and sent a polite follow-up.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I stated the deadline and asked whether everything was all right. She replied on Friday, explained that she had been <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">dealing with</mark> a personal issue, and sent the information. That became the turning point I needed. Instead of pushing harder, I paused and focused on one manageable step at a time. Once I slowed down, I could think more clearly and make fewer mistakes. The improvement was gradual, but my sleep and confidence both recovered. I realised that slowing down does not mean falling behind; steady action can move me forward faster. Since then, when pressure builds, I protect my sleep, reduce unnecessary tasks, and notice ordinary moments. I now feel calmer and enjoy daily life more.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
