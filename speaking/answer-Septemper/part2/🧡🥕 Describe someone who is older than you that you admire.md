## Cue Card

**🧡 Describe someone who is older than you that you admire**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - How you knew this person
> - What kinds of things you like to do together
> - and explain how you feel about this person

> Bank: B4 | Modules: burnout / manageable-steps / boundaries

## 1. Band 7 Answer

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The older person I admire is Stella, my former colleague and one of my best friends. She's about ten years older than me, and we met at work. About three years ago, I moved to Beijing for better job prospects, and we happened to work on the same software project. That's how we got to know each other. The reason I admire her is that she taught me how to protect myself by setting clear boundaries between life and work. About three months ago, our company went through a layoff, and Stella received a much heavier workload. She began sleeping badly, had trouble falling asleep, and even started showing signs of serious burnout. Instead of pretending everything was all right, she honestly talked to her manager and asked him for help. She also pointed out some unrealistic deadlines and stopped replying to non-urgent messages at night. By tackling these issues, she gradually recovered both physically and mentally within a few weeks.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I admire her because she showed me what real strength is. Her success didn't happen overnight. She adjusted because she realized her old habits weren't suitable anymore, so she tried to improve her situation by breaking her challenges into smaller, manageable steps. That's why I admire her. She's a really charming and capable person. Looking back, her experience changed my view of resilience. I used to think strong people just kept working, even under huge pressure. But now, I think strength includes asking for help, accepting support, and changing direction when things don't go as planned. If I face career challenges in the future, I'll learn from her and follow her method: stay calm, take the next actionable step, and most importantly, never treat exhaustion as an achievement.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->

## 2. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: Stella changed her workload, routine, and boundaries instead of pretending the old approach still worked.
- Secondary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: She identified impossible deadlines and divided her recovery into practical steps.

## 3. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my former colleague and one of my best friends, who is about ten years older than me.
- Connection: We got to know each other while working on the same software project after I moved to Beijing three years ago.
- Background: Company layoffs left her with a heavier workload and severe burnout.
- Main event: She spoke to her manager, set clearer boundaries, and rebuilt her health through manageable steps.
- Ability shown: She adapted honestly and solved the causes of the pressure one by one.
- Reflection: I admire her willingness to ask for help and set healthier boundaries; her recovery changed my view of resilience.
