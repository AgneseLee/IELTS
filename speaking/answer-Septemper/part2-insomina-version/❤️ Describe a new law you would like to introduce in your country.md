- Reason: I used what I learned from an ocean documentary to design a focused law with practical alternatives.

- Background: I watched a British ocean documentary after seeing a sea turtle while freediving near Semporna.
- Main event: Coastal businesses would offer reusable alternatives, while tour operators would bring their waste back to shore.
- Ability shown: I turned the programme's explanation of marine plastic into a focused law with practical steps.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The law I'd like to introduce is <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">a ban on single-use plastic</mark> in protected coastal areas. It would ban bags, cups and straws on beaches, marine parks and tourist islands. <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Shops</mark> would offer reusable options, and tour operators would bring waste back to shore. I came up with the law after watching <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">a British online documentary series</mark>. I started watching it after returning from Semporna with Stella, where we saw a sea turtle. One episode <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">explained</mark> how plastic travels from streets and rivers into the sea.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
Most <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">visitors</mark> would support it, although some <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">businesses</mark> might initially <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">object</mark>. The problem initially looked too large for one person to solve. However, this example showed me that rushing or making dramatic promises would not create lasting change. Clear priorities, shared responsibility, and small repeated actions produced a visible result. That changed how I judge progress. I now look for practical steps and feedback instead of expecting an instant solution. The outcome may take time, but steady action is easier to maintain and correct. To me, slow does not mean weak; it means building a result that lasts.
