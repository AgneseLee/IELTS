## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a home that you like to visit but do not want to live in**

> **You should say:**
> - Where it is
> - What it is like
> - Why you like to visit it
> - And explain why you would not like to live there

> Bank: B7 | Modules: high-rise-home / city-view / daily-comfort

> Logic: L1-light · emotional reset: stress → pause → steady action → clearer mind

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: I compared the apartment's attractive view with its everyday drawbacks and identified what I actually need from a home.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella's apartment in a high-rise residential building in central Beijing.
- Background: The building is near the office skyscraper I visit for meetings, so its glass exterior and bright lobby feel familiar.
- Main event: We drink coffee by the window and enjoy the city view, but the packed lifts and poor ventilation make daily life less comfortable.
- Ability shown: I looked past the view and considered how the apartment would feel on an ordinary weekday.
- Reflection: Emotional reset: stress → pause → steady action → clearer mind.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

The home I enjoy visiting is Stella's apartment in<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;"> a high-rise residential building</mark> in central Beijing. I first went there after several sleepless nights caused by work. The quiet visit gave me space to slow down and think clearly. It is near the office tower I visit for meetings, so the area feels familiar. The building has a glass exterior and a bright lobby. Her apartment is on an upper floor with a wide city view and plenty of natural light. I visit every few months. We usually make coffee, sit by the window, and talk.

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
However, the lifts are often packed at rush hour. The ventilation isn’t very good, so the rooms sometimes feel a little <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">stuffy</mark>. That became the turning point I needed. Instead of pushing harder, I paused and focused on one manageable step at a time. Once I slowed down, I could think more clearly and make fewer mistakes. The improvement was gradual, but my sleep and confidence both recovered. I realised that slowing down does not mean falling behind; steady action can move me forward faster. Since then, when pressure builds, I protect my sleep, reduce unnecessary tasks, and notice ordinary moments. I now feel calmer and enjoy daily life more.
