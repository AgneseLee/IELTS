## Cue Card

**Describe one of your friends who learned something without a teacher**

> **You should say:**
> - Who he/she is
> - What he/she learned
> - Why he/she learned this
> - And explain whether it would be easier to learn from a teacher

> Bank: B1 | Modules: online-tutorials / watercolour / recorded-progress

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: Stella chose focused tutorials, practised consistently, sought feedback, and recorded her progress without formal supervision.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my friend and former colleague, who taught herself watercolour painting.
- Background: She wanted a quiet creative outlet after stressful workdays.
- Main event: She used short online tutorials, practised specific techniques, and kept a folder showing her progress.
- Ability shown: She learned independently through clear goals, feedback, and regular practice.
- Reflection: A teacher could correct mistakes faster, but self-study suited her schedule and built self-discipline.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The friend I'd like to describe is Stella, my former colleague. She taught herself watercolour painting because she wanted a quiet creative outlet after stressful workdays. She began with free online tutorials and a small, inexpensive paint set. Rather than watching random videos, she chose one weakness at a time, such as mixing colours or painting light. She practised several times a month and asked online artists for feedback. She also kept a folder of similar scenes painted at different stages, so she could see her progress clearly.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
Learning from a teacher might have been easier. A teacher could notice errors and give her a clear sequence of exercises. However, fixed classes were difficult during busy work periods.

Her self-study shows learning ability because she learned at her own pace without losing direction. She improved through consistent practice, used feedback carefully, and learned from mistakes. Her method changed how I approach technical study. I now record what I build and choose resources for a specific problem instead of collecting endless tutorials. Stella's progress reminds me that independent learning is not simply studying alone. It requires structure, curiosity, and enough self-discipline to continue when nobody is checking. For her, that flexibility made self-teaching more sustainable than formal lessons.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
