## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a person who is good at learning and speaking new languages**

> **You should say:**
> - How you got to know him/her
> - How he/she learns a new language
> - What languages he/she can speak
> - And explain how you feel about him/her

> Bank: B1 | Modules: podcasts / language-exchange / Malaysia-directions

> Logic: L1-light · emotional reset: stress → pause → steady action → clearer mind

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: Stella builds language practice into daily life, records expressions in context, and improves through regular use.
- Secondary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: She prioritises making herself understood, listens actively, and treats mistakes as useful feedback.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my former colleague and friend, who speaks English and conversational Japanese.
- Background: I got to know her about three years ago while working on the same software team.
- Main event: She shadows podcasts daily, attends a weekly language exchange, and used English when we were lost in Malaysia.
- Ability shown: Consistent learning supported by confident, practical communication.
- Reflection: Emotional reset: stress → pause → steady action → clearer mind.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to describe is Stella. When anxiety about English began affecting my sleep, Stella showed me her twenty-minute routine. Her steady method changed how I practised. I met her about three years ago when we worked on the same software team. She speaks English confidently and can also <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">hold conversations in Japanese</mark>. To learn, she listens to short podcasts every morning, shadows the speakers, and records <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">new expressions</mark> in complete sentences. Once a week, she joins an online language exchange. In Malaysia, our app failed, so she asked for directions in English.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
She didn’t worry about making <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">mistakes</mark>. She simply asked for directions, and we soon found our way. That became the turning point I needed. Instead of pushing harder, I paused and focused on one manageable step at a time. Once I slowed down, I could think more clearly and make fewer mistakes. The improvement was gradual, but my sleep and confidence both recovered. I realised that slowing down does not mean falling behind; steady action can move me forward faster. Since then, when pressure builds, I protect my sleep, reduce unnecessary tasks, and notice ordinary moments. I now feel calmer and enjoy daily life more.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
