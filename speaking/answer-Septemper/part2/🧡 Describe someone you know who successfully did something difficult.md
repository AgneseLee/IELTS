## Cue Card

**🧡 Describe someone you know who successfully did something difficult.**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - What difficult thing this person did
> - Why this person was successful
> - How you feel about this person

> Bank: B4 | Modules: burnout / manageable-steps / boundaries

## 1. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to talk about is Stella, my former colleague and one of my best friends. The difficult thing she managed to do was <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">regain control of her work and health</mark> after a period of serious stress. About three months ago, my previous company went through a layoff, and Stella received a much heavier workload. She began sleeping badly at night and even started showing signs of serious burnout and feeling overwhelmed. Instead of pretending everything was all right, she honestly talked to her manager and asked him for help. She also pointed out some unrealistic deadlines and stopped replying to non-urgent messages at night. By tackling these issues, she gradually recovered both physically and mentally within a few weeks.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I was impressed because her success didn't happen overnight. I think she succeeded because she realized her old habits weren't suitable anymore, accepted help, and broke her challenges into smaller steps she could handle. Looking back, her experience changed my view of resilience. I used to think strong people just kept working even under huge pressure, but now I think strength includes asking for help, accepting support, and changing direction when things don't go as planned. If I face career challenges in the future, I'll learn from her and follow her method: stay calm, adapt to the situation, and most importantly, never treat exhaustion as an achievement.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->

## 2. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: Stella changed her workload, routine, and boundaries instead of pretending the old approach still worked.
- Secondary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: She identified impossible deadlines and divided her recovery into practical steps.

## 3. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my former colleague and one of my best friends.
- Background: Company layoffs left her with a heavier workload and severe burnout.
- Main event: She spoke to her manager, set clearer boundaries, and rebuilt her health through manageable steps.
- Ability shown: She adapted honestly and solved the causes of the pressure one by one.
- Reflection: Her recovery taught me that strength includes accepting support and changing an unhealthy approach.
