## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a person you know who would like to choose a career in the medical field (e.g. a doctor, a nurse)**

> **You should say:**
> - When you knew him/her
> - When he/she started to think about that
> - What he/she would like to do
> - And explain why he/she would like to choose this career

> Bank: B8 | Modules: nursing / first-aid / career-motivation

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: Lin studies clinical skills seriously and learns from direct experience caring for patients and family members.
- Secondary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: She explains care calmly and helps frightened people feel safer.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: My cousin Lin, a nursing student who hopes to work in emergency care.
- Background: I have known her since childhood, but she considered nursing during our grandmother's illness.
- Main event: She later handled my cut hand calmly at a family gathering and explained what to watch for.
- Ability shown: She combines continuous learning with clear, reassuring communication.
- Reflection: Her realistic commitment makes me respect both her career choice and the demands of healthcare.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to describe is my cousin Lin, whom I have known since childhood. She first seriously considered a medical career when our grandmother became ill several years ago. Lin helped with her daily care and saw how much calm support mattered. She is now studying nursing and hopes to work in emergency care. At a recent family gathering, I cut my hand while cooking. She cleaned it, checked it carefully, and explained what signs to watch for. Her calm manner immediately made me feel safer.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
What impresses me is that her choice comes from real experience, not an idealised picture of hospital work. She knows the training is demanding, but she still wants to make a meaningful practical difference when people feel vulnerable.

Her studies show strong learning ability. She develops clinical knowledge and learns from every placement. At the same time, she communicates clearly instead of hiding behind medical terms. Watching her changed my perspective on healthcare. Technical skill matters, but trust is built through patience and simple explanations. Lin still has a long way to go, yet she is willing to learn from mistakes and remain calm under pressure. That combination makes nursing a natural career for her.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
