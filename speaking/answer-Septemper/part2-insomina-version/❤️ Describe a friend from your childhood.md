## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a friend from your childhood**

> **You should say:**
> - Who he/she is
> - Where and how you met each other
> - What you often did together
> - And explain what made you like him/her

> Bank: B1 | Modules: school-friend / broken-bicycle / lasting-friendship

> Logic: L1-light · emotional reset: stress → pause → steady action → clearer mind

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: Ming listens without judgment, keeps conversations natural after long gaps, and makes me feel understood.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Ming, my dependable childhood friend from Zhanjiang.
- Background: We met in primary school because we lived in the same neighbourhood and were both quiet children.
- L1 trigger: Stress was affecting sleep, so the experience became a cue to change.
- Main event: We cycled, played badminton, exchanged comic books, and he stayed when my bicycle broke.
- Ability shown: Quiet, trustworthy communication that keeps the friendship secure over time.
- Reflection: Emotional reset: stress → pause → steady action → clearer mind.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The childhood friend I'd like to describe is Ming. Last winter, work pressure was affecting my sleep, so I called Ming. His quiet, dependable support gave me the pause I needed. We met in primary school in Zhanjiang because we lived in the same neighbourhood and were both quiet children. After school, we often cycled along the coast, played badminton, and exchanged comic books. One day, my bicycle chain broke several kilometres from home. Ming did not leave me behind. He helped me push the bicycle and called his father for help.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
That simple moment showed how <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">dependable</mark> he was. We now live in different cities, but we still keep in touch and meet when I return home. That became the turning point I needed. Instead of pushing harder, I paused and focused on one manageable step at a time. Once I slowed down, I could think more clearly and make fewer mistakes. The improvement was gradual, but my sleep and confidence both recovered. I realised that slowing down does not mean falling behind; steady action can move me forward faster. Since then, when pressure builds, I protect my sleep, reduce unnecessary tasks, and notice ordinary moments. I now feel calmer and enjoy daily life more.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
