## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe an important decision that you made**

> **You should say:**
> - What the decision was
> - How you made your decision
> - What the results of the decision were
> - And explain why it was important

> Bank: B4 | Modules: layoff / daily-routine / career-recovery

> Logic: L1-full · emotional recovery: pressure → pause → manageable steps → calmness

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: I analysed a career setback and turned it into a practical plan for retraining and job searching.
- Secondary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: I committed to developing AI skills through regular study and small projects.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: I decided to rebuild my software career after a layoff.
- Background: Losing an eight-year role damaged my confidence and sense of direction.
- Main event: I sought advice, created a routine, and began learning AI tools.
- Ability shown: Informed decision-making and continuous learning.
- Reflection: Emotional recovery: pressure → pause → manageable steps → calmness.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
I decided to rebuild my software career after being laid off from a role. The layoff left me anxious and sleeping badly, so I knew continuing without a plan would make things worse. This decision became my turning point. At first, the setback damaged my <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">confidence</mark>, and I <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">avoided making plans</mark>. After reading a psychology book and talking with my mother and former colleagues, I compared several options. I decided to remain in technology but update my AI skills. I created a weekday <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">routine</mark>, completed structured lessons, built three small projects, and continued applying for suitable roles.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
Several months later, the projects received positive feedback on GitHub. I also had a much clearer <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">career direction</mark>. That became the turning point I needed. Instead of pushing harder, I paused and focused on one manageable step at a time. Once I slowed down, I could think more clearly and make fewer mistakes. The improvement was gradual, but my sleep and confidence both recovered. I realised that slowing down does not mean falling behind; steady action can move me forward faster. Since then, when pressure builds, I protect my sleep, reduce unnecessary tasks, and notice ordinary moments. I now feel calmer and enjoy daily life more.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
