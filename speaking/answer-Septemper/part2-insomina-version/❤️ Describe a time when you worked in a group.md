## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a time when you worked in a group**

> **You should say:**
> - What you did
> - Who you worked with
> - What problems you faced
> - And explain why you worked in the group

> Bank: B4 | Modules: unclear-roles / daily-check-in / completed-project

> Logic: L1-light · emotional reset: stress → pause → steady action → clearer mind

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Leadership & Collaboration
- Reason: I helped four colleagues divide responsibilities and coordinate their work toward a shared deadline.
- Secondary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: Short daily check-ins allowed us to raise problems early and resolve disagreements clearly.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Four colleagues and I built an internal reporting tool.
- Background: The six-week project required different design, coding, and testing skills.
- L1 trigger: Stress was affecting sleep, so the experience became a cue to change.
- Main event: We fixed duplicated work by assigning clear roles and holding daily check-ins.
- Ability shown: Team coordination and open communication.
- Reflection: Emotional reset: stress → pause → steady action → clearer mind.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
I worked in a group of four colleagues at my former software company. As the deadline approached, I was overwhelmed and sleeping badly. The team had to stop rushing and reorganise the work. We had six weeks to build an internal reporting tool, so the project needed design, coding, and testing skills from different people. At first, our <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">roles were unclear</mark>. Two people repeated the same work while an important testing task was ignored. <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Tension</mark> grew as the deadline approached. I suggested dividing the work according to our strengths and holding a ten-minute check-in each morning.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
Stella handled design, I built the system, and the others tested it. We finished <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">on schedule</mark>. That became the turning point I needed. Instead of pushing harder, I paused and focused on one manageable step at a time. Once I slowed down, I could think more clearly and make fewer mistakes. The improvement was gradual, but my sleep and confidence both recovered. I realised that slowing down does not mean falling behind; steady action can move me forward faster. Since then, when pressure builds, I protect my sleep, reduce unnecessary tasks, and notice ordinary moments. I now feel calmer and enjoy daily life more.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
