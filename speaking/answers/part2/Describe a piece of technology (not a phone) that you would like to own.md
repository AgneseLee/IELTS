## Cue Card

**Describe a piece of technology (not a phone) that you would like to own**

> **You should say:**
> - What it is
> - How much it costs
> - How you knew it
> - And explain why you would like to own it

> Bank: B5 | Modules: lightweight-laptop / battery / repairability

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: The laptop would support AI study and let me build demanding projects away from my desk.
- Secondary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: I would compare real performance, repairability, and long-term value before deciding to buy it.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: A lightweight laptop with long battery life and enough power for AI development.
- Background: I learned about it through professional reviews and developer videos.
- Main event: It costs around 15,000 yuan, so I would wait until my current laptop genuinely needs replacement.
- Ability shown: Continuous learning supported by careful purchasing decisions.
- Reflection: I want practical mobility and reliable performance, not a fashionable upgrade.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The technology I'd like to own is a lightweight laptop with long battery life and enough power for AI development. The model I have in mind costs around 15,000 yuan. I first learned about it through professional reviews and videos made by software developers. They tested its battery, coding performance, and weight during real working days. I would use it for programming, AI experiments, English study, and occasional travel. My current laptop still works, so I wouldn't replace it immediately. I would wait for a discount or until the old machine could no longer handle my main tasks.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I want it for practical reasons. A lighter machine and longer battery life would let me work efficiently away from a desk. I could travel without a heavy charger or power worries.

Stronger performance would also support learning. AI projects need more memory and computing power, so fewer interruptions would help me focus on complex tasks.

Even so, I would compare repairability, battery tests, and performance before buying. A heavier laptop might be better if it lasted longer and was easy to repair.

This decision reminds me to separate needs from excitement about new technology. I want the new one only when it becomes a useful professional investment rather than an impulsive upgrade.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
