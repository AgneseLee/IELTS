# government-support-for-artists.md
*2026-07-26*

## Prompt

Some people think that the government should provide assistance to all kinds of artists including painters, musicians and poets. However, other people think that this is a waste of money. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

## Original Draft(315)

The debate surrounding whether the government should assist artists has engaged a broad audience recently. Some people are in favor of it while the others believe that is a waste of money. From my perspective, I am more convinced by the former, as it rests not only more ample theory but also stronger evidence.

Supporters of governments’ assistance put forward several reasons, the most notable being that artistic creations are part of nation’s cultural heritage, and government funding should help with their preservation. Preserving our cultural heritage, including art, music and poets, undoubtedly brings substantial benefits. One key strength is its ability to increase people’s awareness of national history, thereby building a firm basis for the goal of strengthening national identity. As a result, this practice not only generates short-term benefits by creating more art relevant jobs but also contributes to sustainable long-term outcomes, alongside fostering social inclusion. Another notable advantage of this method is that it promote cultural innovation and new forms of artistic creations. With sufficient funding from government, artistic workers are able to work under less pressure without concern about financial responsibilities. That is how this approach creates favorable conditions for further long-term solutions in the artistic field.

However, advocates of the other side may argue that it may pose a risk of wasting social resources, since such a policy requires continuous funding which may place extra burden on taxpayers. Nevertheless, one practical approach to addressing this issue is to make rational taxes ratio through policies and legislation , ensuring more reasonable policy-making. If implemented properly, it can address the problem while preserving its benefits.

In conclusion, although both perspectives have their own merits, I am more convinced by government should support art-related jobs. While the burden of taxpayers can be controlled and alleviated in a certain way, this decision’s advantage overweigh its disadvantage, making our cultural environment more prosperous and lively.

## Revision Advice

### Task Achievement

- [TA] 正方提出了文化遗产、国家认同、就业和创新等理由，但一段中观点过多，部分只点到为止；应保留两条主线并把因果链写完整。
- [TA] 反方只说明持续拨款会增加纳税人负担，随后直接提出含义不清的税收措施，没有解释艺术拨款为何可能挤占医疗、教育等更紧迫的公共服务。
- [TA] 回应反方时，与其写笼统的 “make rational taxes ratio”，不如提出与问题直接对应的保障机制，如 “set clear eligibility criteria and require transparent reporting”。
- [TA] 结论虽然支持前者，但 “support art-related jobs” 缩窄了题目范围；题目讨论的是是否资助艺术家，而不只是就业岗位。

### Coherence and Cohesion

- [CC] 主体段比例失衡：正方约占全文一半，反方不足一个完整论证单元。建议让两个主体段各自包含“观点—解释—结果/例子—判断”。
- [CC] “Nevertheless” 后的措施没有先承认预算取舍，因此反驳显得跳跃。可改为先说明风险成立，再用 “However, this concern does not justify eliminating arts funding altogether” 推进作者判断。
- [CC] “That is how this approach...” 和 “If implemented properly...” 指代宽泛，应明确 “public grants” 或 “transparent funding rules”。

### Lexical Resource

- [LR] “governments’ assistance” → “government support for artists”：原表达所有格和搭配均不自然。
- [LR] “art, music and poets” → “painting, music and poetry”：`poets` 是人，不是文化遗产类别。
- [LR] “art relevant jobs” → “art-related jobs”：复合形容词需要连字符。
- [LR] “artistic workers” → “artists”：后者更自然、准确。
- [LR] “make rational taxes ratio” → “set a reasonable funding limit” / “allocate funds transparently”：原搭配不成立。
- [LR] “overweigh” → “outweigh”：表示“利大于弊”应使用 `outweigh`。
- [LR] “more ample theory” → “stronger reasoning”：`ample theory` 不适合评价一个公众观点的依据。

### Grammatical Range and Accuracy

- [GR] “it promote” → “it promotes” ⚠ recurring：第三人称单数主语后动词加 `-s`。
- [GR] “part of nation’s cultural heritage” → “part of a nation’s cultural heritage”：单数可数名词 `nation` 前需要冠词。
- [GR] “places extra burden” → “places an extra burden”：`burden` 是单数可数名词。
- [GR] “I am more convinced by government should support...” → “I am more convinced that the government should support...”：`convinced by` 后接名词，完整从句应接 `convinced that`。
- [GR] “this decision’s advantage overweigh its disadvantage” → “the advantages of this decision outweigh its disadvantages” ⚠ recurring：需同时修正主谓一致、比较结构和名词单复数。

## Final Integrated Version(273) (7.0–7.5)

People disagree about whether governments should use public money to support painters, musicians, poets and other artists. While such funding can be poorly allocated, I believe that carefully designed assistance is justified because art has cultural value that the market alone may not protect.

Those who favour public support argue that artistic work forms an important part of a nation’s cultural heritage. Commercial demand tends to reward work with mass appeal, whereas historically or culturally significant projects may attract only small audiences. Grants can therefore help preserve painting, music and poetry that might otherwise disappear. They can also give artists enough financial security to experiment with new forms of expression. In turn, a richer cultural environment can strengthen people’s awareness of national history, support cultural identity and create employment in museums, galleries and performances.

Opponents reasonably point out that arts programmes have an opportunity cost. Every grant is financed by taxpayers, and money spent on a project with little artistic or public value cannot be used for healthcare, education or other essential services. Moreover, deciding which artists deserve support is subjective, so an opaque system may favour established institutions or politically acceptable work. However, these risks call for better administration rather than the complete withdrawal of funding. Governments can cap arts budgets, use independent panels, publish selection criteria and require recipients to report how grants are used.

In conclusion, unrestricted subsidies could waste public resources, but this does not make all assistance to artists unjustifiable. Limited and transparent funding can preserve cultural heritage and encourage innovation while keeping the cost to taxpayers under control. I therefore believe that its benefits outweigh its drawbacks.
