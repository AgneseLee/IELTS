## Cue Card

**🧡 Describe someone who is older than you that you admire**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - How you knew this person
> - What kinds of things you like to do together
> - and explain how you feel about this person

> Bank: B4 | Modules: burnout / manageable-steps / boundaries

## 1. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The older person I admire is Stella, my former colleague and one of my best friends. She's about ten years older than me, and we met at work. We often go for walks together and talk about work and life. About three months ago, my previous company went through a layoff, and Stella received a much heavier workload. She began sleeping badly at night and even started showing signs of serious burnout and feeling overwhelmed. Instead of pretending everything was all right, she honestly talked to her manager and asked him for help. She also pointed out some unrealistic deadlines and stopped replying to non-urgent messages at night. By tackling these issues, she gradually recovered both physically and mentally within a few weeks.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I admire her because she showed me that real strength includes asking for help, accepting support, and setting healthier boundaries. Her success didn't happen overnight. She changed because she realized her old habits weren't suitable anymore, and she tried to improve by breaking her challenges into smaller steps she could handle. Looking back, her experience changed my view of resilience. I used to think strong people just kept working even under huge pressure, but now I think strength includes asking for help, accepting support, and changing direction when things don't go as planned. If I face career challenges in the future, I'll learn from her and follow her method: stay calm, adapt to the situation, and most importantly, never treat exhaustion as an achievement.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->

## 2. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: Stella changed her workload, routine, and boundaries instead of pretending the old approach still worked.
- Secondary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: She identified impossible deadlines and divided her recovery into practical steps.

## 3. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my former colleague and one of my best friends, who is about ten years older than me.
- Connection: We met at work and often go for walks together and talk about work and life.
- Background: Company layoffs left her with a heavier workload and severe burnout.
- Main event: She spoke to her manager, set clearer boundaries, and rebuilt her health through manageable steps.
- Ability shown: She adapted honestly and solved the causes of the pressure one by one.
- Reflection: I admire her willingness to ask for help and set healthier boundaries; her recovery changed my view of resilience.
