## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a person who is good at learning and speaking new languages**

> **You should say:**
> - How you got to know him/her
> - How he/she learns a new language
> - What languages he/she can speak
> - And explain how you feel about him/her

> Bank: B1 | Modules: podcasts / language-exchange / Malaysia-directions

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: Stella builds language practice into daily life, records expressions in context, and improves through regular use.
- Secondary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: She prioritises making herself understood, listens actively, and treats mistakes as useful feedback.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my former colleague and friend, who speaks English and conversational Japanese.
- Background: I got to know her about three years ago while working on the same software team.
- Main event: She shadows podcasts daily, attends a weekly language exchange, and used English when we were lost in Malaysia.
- Ability shown: Consistent learning supported by confident, practical communication.
- Reflection: Her method made me value active use more than waiting for perfect grammar.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to describe is Stella, whom I met about three years ago when we worked on the same software team. She speaks English confidently and can also hold conversations in Japanese. To learn, she listens to short podcasts every morning, shadows the speakers, and records new expressions in complete sentences. Once a week, she joins an online language exchange. During our Malaysia trip, our navigation app failed, so she used English to ask other tourists for directions. She focused on making herself understood, and we soon found the correct route.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I admire her discipline and her attitude towards mistakes. She treats them as feedback instead of evidence that she lacks talent.

Her routine shows learning ability because she combines daily exposure, consistent practice, and real communication. It also shows communication skills. She listens actively, adjusts her wording, and cares more about meaning than perfect grammar. Her example changed my English study. I used to collect advanced vocabulary but hesitate when speaking. Now I retrieve familiar phrases under time pressure and request correction only when a mistake blocks meaning. Practice has become more useful. Stella reminds me that confidence does not arrive before we speak; it grows through repeated use. Her progress genuinely motivates me.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
