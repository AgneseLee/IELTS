## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a plan that you had to change recently**

> **You should say:**
> - When this happened
> - What made you change the plan
> - What the new plan was
> - And how you felt about the change

> Bank: B3 | Modules: cancelled-boat / local-market / unexpected-benefit

> Logic: L1-full · emotional recovery: pressure → pause → manageable steps → calmness

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Reason: I accepted unsafe weather conditions and helped replace an island trip with a realistic local activity.
- Secondary Ability: Creativity & Innovation

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella and I changed an island-trip plan in Malaysia.
- Background: Strong wind made the scheduled boat unsafe.
- Main event: We visited a market and cooked a local dish at our accommodation.
- Ability shown: Flexible planning and creative use of available options.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
I had to change a plan during my Malaysia trip with Stella last May. I was already exhausted and sleeping badly before the plan failed. The cancellation forced me to pause instead of controlling every detail. We had booked a boat to visit an island, but strong wind made <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">the sea unsafe</mark>, so the operator cancelled it that morning. I was <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">disappointed</mark> because the island was one of the main reasons for our trip. Stella suggested visiting a local market instead and cooking a Malaysian dish at our accommodation.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
We bought <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">unfamiliar spices</mark>, asked a vendor for advice, and experimented with the recipe. The meal was imperfect but surprisingly tasty, and the vendor's friendly help made the afternoon memorable. That became the turning point I needed. Instead of pushing harder, I paused and focused on one manageable step at a time. Once I slowed down, I could think more clearly and make fewer mistakes. The improvement was gradual, but my sleep and confidence both recovered. I realised that slowing down does not mean falling behind; steady action can move me forward faster. Since then, when pressure builds, I protect my sleep, reduce unnecessary tasks, and notice ordinary moments. I now feel calmer and enjoy daily life more.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
