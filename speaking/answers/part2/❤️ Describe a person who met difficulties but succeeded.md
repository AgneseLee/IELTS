## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a person who met difficulties but succeeded**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - What difficulties he met
> - How he overcame the difficulties
> - And explain how you feel about him

> Bank: B4 | Modules: burnout / manageable-steps / healthier-role

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: Stella changed her workload, routine, and boundaries instead of pretending the old approach still worked.
- Secondary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: She identified impossible deadlines and divided her recovery into practical steps.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my former colleague and close friend.
- Background: Company layoffs left her with a heavier workload and severe burnout.
- Main event: She spoke to her manager, set clearer boundaries, and rebuilt her health through manageable steps.
- Ability shown: She adapted honestly and solved the causes of the pressure one by one.
- Reflection: Her recovery taught me that strength includes accepting support and changing an unhealthy approach.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to describe is Stella, my former colleague and close friend. After our company went through layoffs, she received a much heavier workload. She began sleeping badly, losing confidence, and showing signs of serious burnout. Instead of pretending everything was fine, she spoke honestly with her manager. She identified deadlines that were impossible, stopped answering non-urgent messages at night, and divided her recovery into manageable steps. She also took short evening walks and accepted help from colleagues. Several months later, her sleep improved and she moved into a healthier role.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I feel impressed because her success was not overnight. It came from recognising that her old approach was harmful and adjusting it consistently.

The experience showed adaptability. Stella could not undo the layoffs, but she could focus on what she could control and create clearer boundaries. It also showed problem-solving because she addressed the causes of burnout instead of hiding the symptoms. Her example changed my view of resilience. I used to think strong people simply continued under pressure. Now I think strength includes accepting support and changing direction when a situation becomes unhealthy. When I face career stress, I follow her method: stay calm, identify the next useful action, and never treat exhaustion as achievement.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
