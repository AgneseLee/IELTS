## Cue Card

**Describe something important that has been kept in your family for a long time**

> **You should say:**
> - What it is
> - When your family had it
> - How your family got it
> - And explain why it is important f or your family

> Bank: B2 | Modules: childhood-essay / mother / emotional-value

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: The childhood essay recorded feelings I was too shy to express clearly in everyday conversation.
- Secondary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: Reading it years later helped me recognise how my mother's support shaped my confidence.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: A handwritten primary-school essay about my mother.
- Background: I wrote it almost twenty years ago for a class assignment.
- Main event: My teacher returned it, and my mother quietly stored it with family papers.
- Ability shown: Expressing feelings in writing and learning from an old memory.
- Reflection: Its ordinary paper carries a family history that a replacement could not reproduce.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The object is a handwritten essay about my mother that I wrote in primary school. It has been in my family for almost twenty years. A teacher asked our class to describe someone important, so I wrote about my mother's patience and practical advice. After marking it, the teacher returned the paper to me. My mother read it and quietly stored it with other family documents. I had completely forgotten about it until she found it again last year. The paper is ordinary, and my handwriting is untidy. It includes spelling mistakes and a small drawing beside the title.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
The essay preserves a childhood version of our relationship. I was shy, so writing helped me express feelings I rarely said aloud. Reading it now brings back warm memories.

Her advice seemed ordinary then, but I now recognise how often she helped me solve problems independently.

When we reread it together, we discussed my old teacher and how difficult speaking up once felt. That conversation connected two stages of my life through one piece of paper.

I would never sell or replace it. Its value doesn't come from age, price, or perfect writing. It carries emotional value because my mother saved it without knowing whether it would matter later.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
