## Cue Card

**Describe a child who loves drawing/painting**

> **You should say:**
> - Who he/she is
> - How/when you knew him/her
> - How often he/she draws/paints
> - And explain why you think he/she loves drawing/painting

> Bank: B1 | Modules: Mia / park-painting / creative-freedom

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Creativity & Innovation
- Reason: Mia changes familiar scenes through colour and imagined details to express how they feel to her.
- Secondary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: She draws several times a week, notices details, and calmly identifies what she wants to improve.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Mia, my eight-year-old niece, whom I have known since she was born.
- Background: She began drawing seriously after receiving a small watercolour set for her birthday.
- Main event: She paints several times a week and created an imaginative version of a park we visited.
- Ability shown: Creative experimentation supported by observation and regular practice.
- Reflection: She loves the freedom to choose colours, change reality, and tell a story without adult control.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The child I'd like to describe is my eight-year-old niece, Mia, whom I have known since she was born. She became serious about drawing after receiving a small watercolour set for her birthday. Now she draws or paints several times a week, usually animals, flowers, and places she has visited. Last year, after a family walk, she painted the park we had seen. Instead of copying a photograph, she added brighter flowers and a small imaginary bridge. The picture was not technically perfect, but it captured the peaceful feeling of that afternoon.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
Mia loves painting because it gives her creative freedom. She can choose colours, change reality, and express feelings in her own way.

Her habit shows creativity and learning ability. She explores new possibilities, yet patiently practises and observes ordinary details. When she gave the park painting to her grandmother, she felt proud but mentioned one improvement. Watching her changed my perspective on children's creativity. It grows best with encouragement and freedom to experiment, rather than constant comparison. Our family asks about the story behind each picture instead of judging it. This keeps the hobby enjoyable and helps Mia learn from experience. Her enthusiasm reminds me that creativity requires imagination and courage.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
