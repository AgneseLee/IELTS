## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe a person who loves to grow plants (e.g. vegetables, flowers) at home or in the garden**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - What plants he/she grows
> - How he/she grows the plants
> - And explain why he/she loves growing plants

> Bank: B8 | Modules: balcony-garden / rain-damage / daily-routine

> Logic: L1-light · emotional reset: stress → pause → steady action → clearer mind

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: After heavy rain damaged her tomatoes, my mother moved the pots indoors and changed how often she watered them.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: My mother, a retired accountant living in Zhanjiang.
- Background: She grows spring onions, lettuce and tomatoes in recycled containers on her balcony.
- L1 trigger: Stress was affecting sleep, so the experience became a cue to change.
- Main event: She saved most of her tomato plants after heavy rain soaked the soil and damaged their leaves.
- Ability shown: She observed how the plants responded and adjusted their location and watering routine.
- Reflection: Emotional reset: stress → pause → steady action → clearer mind.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to describe is my mother, a retired accountant in Zhanjiang. During a stressful period when I was sleeping badly, my mother invited me to help on her balcony. Her patient routine became a turning point. She has turned her <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">small balcony</mark> into a vegetable garden. She grows spring onions, lettuce, and tomatoes in <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">recycled containers</mark>. Every morning, she checks the leaves, feels the soil, and waters the plants carefully. She also avoids strong chemicals. Last summer, <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">heavy rain</mark> soaked the soil and damaged several tomato plants.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
Instead of throwing them away, she moved the pots indoors, removed the broken leaves, and watered them less often. Most plants <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">recovered</mark>, and we later ate the tomatoes together. That became the turning point I needed. Instead of pushing harder, I paused and focused on one manageable step at a time. Once I slowed down, I could think more clearly and make fewer mistakes. The improvement was gradual, but my sleep and confidence both recovered. I realised that slowing down does not mean falling behind; steady action can move me forward faster. Since then, when pressure builds, I protect my sleep, reduce unnecessary tasks, and notice ordinary moments. I now feel calmer and enjoy daily life more.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
