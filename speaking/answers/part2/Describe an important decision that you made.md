## Cue Card

**Describe an important decision that you made** ❤️

> **You should say:**
> - What the decision was
> - How you made your decision
> - What the results of the decision were
> - And explain why it was important

> Bank: B4 | Modules: layoff / daily-routine / career-recovery

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: I analysed a career setback and turned it into a practical plan for retraining and job searching.
- Secondary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: I committed to developing AI skills through regular study and small projects.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: I decided to rebuild my software career after a layoff.
- Background: Losing an eight-year role damaged my confidence and sense of direction.
- Main event: I sought advice, created a routine, and began learning AI tools.
- Ability shown: Informed decision-making and continuous learning.
- Reflection: The decision changed an uncontrollable setback into a direction I could influence.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The important decision I made was to rebuild my software career after I was laid off from a role I had held for years. At first, the setback damaged my confidence, and I avoided making plans. After reading a psychology book and talking with my mother and former colleagues, I compared several options. I decided to remain in technology but update my AI skills. I created a weekday routine, completed structured lessons, built small projects, and continued applying for suitable roles. Several months later, I could discuss new tools confidently and had a much clearer career direction.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
The decision turned something outside my control into a problem I could act on. I identified practical actions and measured progress through completed tasks rather than job offers.

It also renewed my learning ability. Becoming a beginner again was uncomfortable, but steady practice helped me regain my confidence. I learned that one employer's decision does not define my value and abilities develop over time. I still face uncertainty, but now have clear evidence of progress. This personal turning point gave me responsibility for my future direction without pretending the setback was easy. It made uncertainty feel more manageable, constructive, and purposeful in my everyday working life.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
