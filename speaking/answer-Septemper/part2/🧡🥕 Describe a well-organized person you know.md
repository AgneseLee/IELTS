## Cue Card

**🧡 Describe a well-organized person you know**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - What this person usually does to stay organized
> - In what situations you have noticed this quality
> - And explain why you think being organized is important to him/her

> Bank: B4 | Modules: priorities / manageable-steps / boundaries

## 1. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to talk about is Stella, my former colleague and one of my best friends. 

I noticed these habits during our conversations about work after my previous company went through a layoff about three months ago. Stella received a much heavier workload. She began sleeping badly at night and feeling overwhelmed. To stay organized, she writes down her main priorities each morning and breaks larger tasks into smaller steps she can handle. She also leaves some time for unexpected work instead of filling every minute of her day. She then organized her daily schedule around those priorities and stopped replying to non-urgent messages at night. By tackling these issues, she gradually recovered both physically and mentally within a few weeks.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I think being organized is important to her because it helps her manage a demanding job while protecting her health. Having clear priorities means she knows what to focus on and what can wait, so she feels more in control of her work.

Looking back, her experience changed my view of being organized. I used to think it simply meant fitting more tasks into a day, but now I think it includes setting realistic goals, accepting support, and changing plans when things don't go as expected. I admire her because she learned to work in a healthier way. If I face career challenges in the future, I'll follow her method: focus on what I can control, take one manageable step at a time, and never treat exhaustion as an achievement.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->

## 2. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: Stella identifies urgent tasks, sets daily priorities, and breaks larger tasks into manageable steps.
- Secondary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: She adjusted her schedule and boundaries when her increased workload affected her health.

## 3. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my former colleague and one of my best friends.
- Daily habits: She writes down priorities, breaks larger tasks into smaller steps, and leaves time for unexpected work.
- Background: Company layoffs left her with a heavier workload, poor sleep, and a sense of being overwhelmed.
- Main event: In our conversations about work, I noticed how she worked with her manager to prioritize tasks, reorganized her schedule, and set healthier boundaries.
- Ability shown: Practical planning and willingness to adapt an unhealthy routine.
- Reflection: Being organized helps her manage demanding work while protecting her health; it means making realistic plans rather than filling every minute.
