## Cue Card

**Describe an occasion when you were not allowed to use your mobile phone**

> **You should say:**
> - When it was
> - Where it was
> - Why you were not allowed to use your mobile phone
> - And how you felt about it

> Bank: B7 | Modules: museum-rule / close-attention / justified-limit

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: I accepted a temporary museum restriction and changed how I observed and remembered the exhibition.
- Secondary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: Focused attention helped me notice details and understand how phone-free viewing affected my experience.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: A phone-free special exhibition at a Beijing art museum.
- Background: Sensitive works required protection from photography and digital distraction.
- Main event: I stored my phone, observed closely, and discussed the paintings with Stella.
- Ability shown: Adapting to a restriction and learning through direct attention.
- Reflection: The rule felt worthwhile once I experienced deeper engagement with the art.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The occasion was a special art exhibition at a museum in Beijing last year. I went with Stella, and visitors had to silence and store their phones before entering. The staff explained that photography could damage some sensitive works and that bright screens would disturb the quiet atmosphere. At first, I felt inconvenienced because I normally use my phone for notes and pictures. However, without notifications, I paid much closer attention to the paintings and discussed their details with Stella. One large landscape held our attention for several minutes, which rarely happens when I can check messages.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
By the end, the restriction felt reasonable. It protected the artwork and gave me a genuine break from my busy daily routine.

The occasion improved my adaptability because I changed my behaviour instead of resisting a clear rule. It also became a learning experience: describing paintings aloud created stronger memories than photographs I rarely view. Looking back, technology is useful, but constant access can weaken concentration. I would not ban phones in every museum because some visitors need translation or accessibility tools. Here, though, the limit created practical comfort and helped me clear my head. The art became the main experience rather than content for a post.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
