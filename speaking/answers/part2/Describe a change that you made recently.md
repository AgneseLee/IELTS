## Cue Card

**Describe a change that you made recently** ❤️

> **You should say:**
> - What the change was
> - What caused the change
> - What you did for the change
> - And explain how you feel about the change

> Bank: B4 | Modules: layoff / daily-routine / manageable-steps

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: I replaced an exhausting job-search routine with a healthier structure after noticing it was unsustainable.
- Secondary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: I divided a vague career problem into small daily actions that I could control.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: I changed my daily routine while rebuilding my career.
- Background: Constant job searching after a layoff left me tired and unfocused.
- Main event: I added a morning walk and limited each weekday to three priority tasks.
- Ability shown: I adjusted my approach and created practical steps.
- Reflection: The change improved both my concentration and mental health.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The recent change I made was rebuilding my weekday routine after I lost my software job. At first, I searched for work from morning until night because being busy made me feel responsible. After several unproductive weeks, however, I was tired and could not concentrate. I replaced that routine with three manageable steps: one technical lesson, one job application, and one English session. I also added a thirty-minute morning walk and stopped checking job messages late at night. Within a few weeks, I was completing tasks more consistently and sleeping better.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I feel genuinely positive about the change because it gave structure to a period that felt uncertain. More importantly, it improved my adaptability. I did not change my goal; I adjusted my approach when the old method stopped working. The routine also strengthened my problem-solving skills because I learned to focus on what I could control instead of worrying about every possible result.

Looking back, constant effort is not always effective effort. Rest and exercise now support my progress rather than compete with it. I would keep this routine even after finding full-time work. The change helped me regain confidence and showed me that a difficult transition becomes manageable through practical daily actions.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
