## Final Integrated Version(175) (7.0–7.5)

The diagram illustrates how a hydroelectric power station generates electricity through a daily cycle involving two reservoirs.

Overall, the system operates differently during the day and at night. In daytime, water flows downhill from the high-level reservoir to generate electricity before entering the low-level reservoir; at night, it is pumped back uphill, completing the cycle.

During the day, river water is stored behind a dam in the upper reservoir. Its intake is opened, allowing water to travel down a pipe to the power station. The flow turns reversible turbines, which drive a generator. Electricity produced by this equipment is then transmitted along power lines to the national grid, while the water is discharged into the lower reservoir.

At night, by contrast, the intake at the dam is closed and electricity is not generated. The same turbines operate in reverse as pumps, drawing water from the low-level reservoir and forcing it back through the pipe to the high-level one. Water is therefore restored to its original position, ready for the daytime generation process to begin again.

### Minimally Adjusted Version(184) (Target Band 7.0–7.5)

The diagram illustrates how a hydroelectric power station generates electricity.

Overall, **this is a cyclical process with contrasting daytime and nighttime phases. During the day, water moves from a high-level reservoir to a low-level one while electricity is generated; at night, the water is pumped in the opposite direction, completing the cycle.**

**During the day, river water enters and is stored in the high-level reservoir behind the dam. The intake is opened, allowing water to flow downhill through a pipe to the power station.
