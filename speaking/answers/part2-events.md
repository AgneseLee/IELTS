## 1. Getting up early

### Keywords and collocations

Malaysia · sunrise boat · 4:30 a.m. · limited sleep · quiet harbour · breathtaking view · worth the effort · lasting memory

### Complete answer

The occasion took place during my Malaysia trip with Stella. We had booked an early boat to watch the sunrise near Semporna, so I had to get up at 4:30 a.m. I normally struggle with early mornings and initially felt **half awake and slightly irritable**. However, the harbour was unusually quiet, and the cool air gradually woke me up. Once the boat reached open water, the sky changed from dark blue to orange and the sea reflected the light. The view was **far more impressive than I had expected**. We took only a few photographs and spent most of the time enjoying the silence. I was tired later, but the experience was completely **worth the effort**. It taught me that getting up early can reveal a side of a place that most visitors miss.

### Part 3

**Q: Why do people get up early?** A: Usually for work, exercise or travel. Early hours provide **fewer distractions and more control over the day**.

**Q: What occasions require an early arrival?** A: Flights, interviews, examinations and medical appointments because lateness can **create serious consequences**.

**Q: Why do some people stay up late?** A: They may work irregular hours or seek quiet personal time, although the habit can **disrupt sleep quality**.

**Q: Is arriving early always good?** A: Usually, but arriving excessively early may inconvenience a host. People should use **reasonable judgement**.

## 3. Working in a group

### Keywords and collocations

software project · four colleagues · tight deadline · unclear roles · divide responsibilities · daily check-in · resolve disagreement · complementary strengths

### Complete answer

The group experience I remember was a software project at my former company. Four of us had six weeks to build an internal reporting tool. Initially, responsibilities were unclear, so two people worked on the same feature while another important task was ignored. Tension began to rise because the deadline was approaching. I suggested that we **divide the work according to individual strengths** and hold a ten-minute check-in each morning. Stella handled design, I built the main system, and the others tested data and user access. The short meetings revealed problems early without wasting much time. We completed the project on schedule, and staff used it to reduce repetitive work. The experience showed me that teamwork is valuable when communication is clear; otherwise, a group can **create duplication rather than efficiency**.

### Part 3

**Q: Why do some people work alone?** A: They value concentration and control and may dislike waiting for group decisions. Individual work suits tasks requiring **deep, uninterrupted attention**.

**Q: What should a leader do?** A: Set clear goals, listen to concerns and distribute work fairly. Trust grows when decisions are **consistent and transparent**.

**Q: Should students learn group work?** A: Yes, because adult life requires negotiation and shared responsibility. Assessment should still prevent **unequal participation**.

**Q: What group tasks exist at school?** A: Experiments, presentations, sports and community projects, all of which require students to **coordinate different roles**.
