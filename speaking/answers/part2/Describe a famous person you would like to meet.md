## Cue Card

**Describe a famous person you would like to meet**

> **You should say:**
> - Who he/she is
> - How you knew him/her
> - How/where you would like to meet him/her
> - And explain why you would like to meet him/her

> Bank: B5 | Modules: reusable-rockets / failed-tests / engineering-question

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Creativity & Innovation
- Reason: I want to understand how Elon Musk's teams turn unconventional engineering ideas into working systems.
- Secondary Ability: Learning Ability
- Reason: I would ask how useful evidence from repeated failures guides the next decision.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Elon Musk, a well-known technology entrepreneur linked with SpaceX.
- Background: I first learned about him through a documentary on reusable rockets.
- Main event: I would meet him at an engineering event and ask about ambitious projects, failed tests, and decision-making.
- Ability shown: The discussion would explore innovation supported by testing and continuous learning.
- Reflection: I am interested in the reasoning behind difficult projects, not fame itself.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The famous person I'd like to meet is Elon Musk. I first learned about him through a documentary about SpaceX and reusable rockets. It showed several failed tests before a successful landing, which made the engineering process memorable. If I had the chance, I would meet him at a small engineering conference rather than a celebrity event. I would ask how he decides whether an ambitious idea is realistic, how he responds when a test fails, and how he keeps a technical team focused during a long project.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I would not meet him simply because he is famous, and I do not agree with every opinion. What interests me is how unconventional ideas become working systems through evidence and repeated improvement.

As a software engineer, I face smaller versions of the same challenge. A bold idea is exciting, but it still needs testing, feedback, and practical limits. I want to learn how an experienced leader responds to failure without abandoning a difficult goal. I would also ask about the human cost of ambitious deadlines, because innovation should not ignore the people doing the work. Even if I disagreed with him, hearing his reasoning could help me judge technical claims carefully and explore new possibilities with a balanced mindset.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
