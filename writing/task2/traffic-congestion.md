## Final Version (7.0-7.5)
**Word count: 307**

The debate surrounding traffic congestion has engaged a broad audience recently. This problem can be attributed to multiple causes, and both governments and individuals can take feasible steps to alleviate it. This essay will examine the main causes of this issue and propose possible solutions.

One major reason behind traffic congestion in cities is the lack of a reliable and well-connected public transport network. When buses, underground systems and other forms of public transport are inconvenient or poorly connected, citizens often have to spend more time commuting and transferring between different modes of transport. The situation becomes worse when more people choose to drive private vehicles, as this places additional pressure on already crowded roads and reinforces a pattern that is difficult to change. Another contributing factor is poor public awareness of traffic rules. If drivers ignore lane discipline, parking restrictions or traffic signals, they may cause accidents and unnecessary delays, which further reduces road efficiency.

Nevertheless, although the problem appears severe, it is far from unsolvable. One practical approach is to promote the use of public transport. Governments could introduce regulations to restrict private vehicles on main roads during peak hours, especially non-local cars in the busiest urban areas. This would be effective because it directly improves urban mobility and commuting efficiency, ensuring that commuters spend less time travelling than before. Another important step is to raise public awareness of traffic rules. The key reason is that this not only promotes road safety but also improves the efficiency of city roads by reducing traffic accidents and avoidable delays. As a result, this strategy can reduce immediate pressure on transport systems and ensure more lasting outcomes.

In conclusion, urban traffic congestion can be traced back to several causes, such as insufficient public transport and poor public awareness of traffic rules. Nevertheless, by adopting effective measures at both governmental and individual levels, including promoting public transport and improving people’s awareness, its negative impact can be mitigated in the long run.

### 词汇误用

| 错误 | 修正 | 原因 |
|---|---|---|
| `public transportation network` | `public transport network` | Both are possible, but `public transport network` is more natural in IELTS-style writing. |
| `transportation tools` | `modes of transport` | Buses and underground systems are transport modes, not tools. |
| `by their private vehicles` | `by private vehicles` / `drive private vehicles` | The original phrase is unnatural. |
| `release some regulations` | `introduce regulations` | Regulations are normally `introduced`, not `released`, in this context. |
| `regarding promotion of public transportation` | `to encourage the use of public transport` | The revised phrase is clearer and more natural. |
| `commuting costs less time` | `commuters spend less time travelling` | Time is usually expressed with `take` or `spend`, not `cost`, in this context. |
| `traffic jam` | `traffic congestion` | `Traffic congestion` is more suitable for formal Task 2 writing. |
| `citizen's awareness` | `public awareness` / `citizens' awareness` | The essay refers to citizens in general, so the singular possessive is incorrect. |

### 结构改进

The original essay has a usable causes-and-solutions structure, but the development of ideas is uneven. The revised version keeps the same main ideas while making each logic chain clearer: poor public transport increases reliance on private cars, and poor awareness of traffic rules causes accidents and delays. The solutions paragraph is also more specific by linking vehicle restrictions and public awareness campaigns directly to urban mobility, road safety and commuting efficiency.
