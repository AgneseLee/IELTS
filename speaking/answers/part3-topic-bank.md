# IELTS Speaking Part 3 主题逻辑答案库

> 观点来源：[`../../writing/task2/views-v2.md`](../../writing/task2/views-v2.md)。本文件只把已有 Task 2 逻辑链转成自然口语，并将它们重组到 Part 3 高频入口中。

## 使用方法

- 不逐字背答案。每题只记 `立场 → 原因/机制 → 结果 → 限定`。
- 目标时长为 45–60 秒；练习时先说加粗逻辑，再补例子。
- 同一条逻辑链要练习换题迁移。例如“资源公平 → 机会公平 → 社会流动”可用于教育、住房、医疗和儿童话题。
- 示例用于说明机制，不必声称是统计事实。考场上可以换成自己熟悉的中国城市、学校或公司。

## 七种常见追问维度

| 维度 | 快速起点 | 展开重点 |
|---|---|---|
| 原因 Why | `The main reason is that...` | 找到行为背后的激励、需求或限制 |
| 群体差异 | `It depends largely on...` | 比较年龄、收入、教育或生活阶段 |
| 今昔变化 | `Compared with the past...` | 说明技术、收入或制度如何改变行为 |
| 未来预测 | `I think this trend will...` | 给趋势，也给制约条件 |
| 利弊评价 | `On balance, I think...` | 主立场展开两步，再承认一个限制 |
| 社会影响 | `At a wider level...` | 从个人结果上升到公平、信任或生产力 |
| 谁应负责 | `Responsibility should be shared...` | 政府管制度，机构管执行，个人管选择 |

### 维度练习导航

- **原因：** AI 使用、虚假信息传播、居民反感旅游、职场倦怠。
- **群体差异：** 技术是否公平、不同收入群体的健康、代际压力、广告对年轻人的影响。
- **今昔变化：** 媒体角色、家庭中的科技、城市化与工作方式。
- **未来预测：** 数字隐私、本地文化、可持续旅游、休闲时间。
- **利弊评价：** 政府监管、全球化、跨文化接触、远程办公、儿童竞争。
- **社会影响：** 教育公平、收入差距、消费、犯罪预防。
- **谁应负责：** 儿童行为、环境保护、住房、公共体育设施。

不需要为每个主题分别背七套答案。练习时把某个主题的“复用逻辑”放进不同维度即可。

# 第一部分：12 个核心主题

## 01 教育

**复用逻辑：** 公平投入 → 缩小学习差距 → 机会更公平；职业教育 → 岗位技能 → 就业能力；考试导向 → 挤压探索 → 创造力下降。

### Q1 Why is equal access to education important?

I'd say it matters because education strongly affects a person's future opportunities. If disadvantaged students can access good teachers and facilities, the learning gap becomes smaller. They then have a fairer chance of entering university or finding stable work. For example, online courses can give students in remote areas access to specialist teaching. Of course, access alone does not guarantee success. Students still need effort and family support. Overall, fair educational investment can improve social mobility instead of allowing family background to decide everything.

### Q2 Do schools focus too much on examinations?

In many cases, yes. The main problem is that schools are judged by test results, so teachers naturally spend more time on exam techniques. This can reduce time for experiments, discussion and open-ended projects. As a result, students may become good at remembering information but less confident about questioning it. Standardised tests are still useful because they provide a common measure. However, they should be combined with practical work and continuous assessment. That would protect fairness without weakening independent thinking and creativity.

### Q3 Should schools or families teach children good behaviour?

Responsibility should be shared. Families shape children's earliest habits because parents are their first role models. Schools then give them a wider social environment where they learn discipline, empathy and cooperation. For instance, a child may learn honesty at home but practise teamwork during a school project. If the two sides send completely different messages, children can become confused. So schools should explain clear standards, while parents reinforce them through daily behaviour. Together, they can build civic responsibility and stronger social cohesion.

## 02 科技

**复用逻辑：** 自动化 → 高价值工作 → 生产力；远程服务 → 缩小地域差距；数据收集 → 隐私受损；岗位替代 → 收入差距。

### Q1 Why do companies increasingly use AI?

The main reason is efficiency. AI can handle repetitive tasks quickly and consistently, so employees can focus on work requiring judgement or creativity. This increases the amount of useful work produced in the same period. For example, software can summarise routine reports before a manager reviews the important decisions. However, companies should not treat AI as a perfect replacement for people. Human checking is still essential when a decision affects customers or employees. Used carefully, AI can raise productivity without removing human responsibility.

### Q2 Does technology benefit everyone equally?

Not necessarily. Online education and telemedicine can greatly help people in remote areas because distance becomes less important. However, these benefits depend on reliable internet access, suitable devices and digital skills. Low-income or older users may struggle with those requirements. At the same time, automation can replace routine jobs faster than some workers can retrain. This may widen income inequality. So technology has enormous potential, but governments and employers need to provide infrastructure and training if they want its benefits to be widely shared.

### Q3 Will people care more about digital privacy in the future?

I think they will, especially as more services collect personal information. People are becoming aware that data can be leaked, sold or used to influence their choices. Once users lose trust, they may avoid otherwise useful digital services. For example, someone may hesitate to use a health app if its privacy policy is unclear. That said, convenience often wins in daily life, so awareness alone is insufficient. Stronger regulation and simpler privacy controls will be needed to turn public concern into genuine protection.

## 03 社会

**复用逻辑：** 社会保障 → 降低贫困 → 社会稳定；基本服务 → 缩小机会差距；收入差距 → 社会排斥；福利设计不当 → 就业动力下降。

### Q1 Why should society support vulnerable people?

From my perspective, basic support protects both individuals and society. When vulnerable people can afford food, housing and medical treatment, they are less likely to fall into extreme poverty. This gives them a better chance of returning to education or employment. At a wider level, lower poverty can improve social stability and trust. However, support should not simply be unlimited cash payments. It should include useful services and a sensible path back to work. That protects dignity while keeping the system financially sustainable.

### Q2 How does income inequality affect society?

Wide income gaps can gradually weaken social cohesion. Low-income families often have poorer access to education, healthcare and digital services. Their children therefore have fewer opportunities to move upwards, and disadvantage can continue across generations. People may then feel that the system is unfair, which reduces social trust. Differences in income are not automatically harmful because skills and effort should be rewarded. The real problem arises when basic opportunities depend almost entirely on wealth. Public services can help prevent that outcome.

### Q3 Can generous welfare discourage people from working?

It can, but the design matters more than the existence of welfare. If someone loses all benefits immediately after taking a low-paid job, working may bring almost no financial improvement. That creates a weak incentive to seek employment. A better system would reduce support gradually as earnings rise. It could also provide childcare and job training. In that way, vulnerable people keep basic security while gaining a clear reward from work. So the solution is careful benefit withdrawal, not simply cutting welfare.

## 04 政府

**复用逻辑：** 公共投资 → 人力资本 → 长期生产力；环保法规 → 企业减排；过度干预 → 创新下降；监管不力 → 预算挤压。

### Q1 What services should governments prioritise?

I'd prioritise education and healthcare because both strengthen human capital. Better schools give people useful skills, while accessible healthcare keeps them healthy enough to study and work. These benefits raise long-term productivity rather than solving only a temporary problem. For example, preventive healthcare can reduce expensive complications later. Governments still need to fund transport, security and environmental protection, so priorities will vary by place. However, education and healthcare create especially broad benefits and give disadvantaged groups a fairer starting point.

### Q2 Is government regulation always beneficial?

No. Regulation is necessary when private actions create public harm, such as pollution or unsafe products. Clear environmental rules can make companies reduce emissions and protect public health. However, excessive or confusing regulation raises administrative costs and can discourage smaller firms from innovating. The key is proportionality. Rules should target a specific risk, be easy to understand and be reviewed regularly. Good regulation protects the public while preserving competition; poor regulation creates paperwork without producing a meaningful social benefit.

### Q3 Why do some public projects waste money?

One important factor is weak procurement and oversight. If contracts are awarded without proper checks, projects may be delayed or completed below standard. The government then has to pay for repairs, and the budget rises further. This also means less money is available for schools, hospitals or other services. Large projects can face genuine unexpected problems, so not every delay shows misconduct. Still, transparent bidding, clear responsibilities and independent inspection can reduce avoidable waste and maintain public trust.

## 05 媒体

**复用逻辑：** 信息渠道 → 公众知情；独立调查 → 问责；流量激励 → 虚假信息；个人数据 → 定向广告与冲动消费。

### Q1 Why does false information spread quickly online?

The main reason is that online platforms compete for attention. Sensational claims often produce more clicks and comments than careful explanations, so algorithms give them greater exposure. Users with limited media literacy may then share the content without checking it. This can distort public opinion very quickly. Traditional media can also make mistakes, but online distribution gives a false claim unusual speed and scale. Platforms should reduce obvious manipulation, while schools should teach people how to compare sources and verify claims.

### Q2 How has the media's role changed?

Compared with the past, people now receive news from a much wider range of sources. Information spreads almost instantly, which can make the public better informed and increase social transparency. Independent journalists and ordinary citizens can also expose misconduct by governments or companies. However, the old editorial filters have become weaker. Reliable reporting now competes directly with rumours and emotionally charged posts. The media has therefore become more open and powerful, but individuals need stronger judgement when deciding what to trust.

### Q3 Should targeted advertising be restricted?

I think it should be regulated, particularly when companies use sensitive personal data. Repeated personalised advertisements can exploit a person's habits and encourage impulse purchases. Some consumers may buy products they cannot really afford, leading to debt and stress. Targeting can still be useful because people see more relevant offers and small firms reach suitable customers. A complete ban would be excessive. Clear consent, limits for children and easy ways to disable tracking would create a fairer balance.

## 06 国际

**复用逻辑：** 贸易与知识交流 → 创新增长；协定共享资源 → 应对危机；经济依赖 → 外部冲击；全球文化 → 本地认同减弱。

### Q1 Why is international cooperation important?

Many modern problems cross national borders, so one country cannot solve them alone. During a health or environmental crisis, agreements allow governments to share information, specialists and essential supplies. This can make assistance faster and reduce casualties. Cooperation also spreads technology and knowledge between economies. Of course, countries have different interests, and agreements can be slow. Even so, a limited coordinated response is usually more effective than isolated action when the cause and consequences are genuinely international.

### Q2 Does globalisation help all countries equally?

No, the benefits are uneven. Global trade allows firms to reach larger markets and adopt new technology, which can raise production and innovation. However, weaker economies may become highly dependent on foreign investment or a small number of export markets. An external shock can then damage local businesses and employment very quickly. Globalisation creates opportunities, but countries still need diverse industries, worker training and basic social protection. Those policies help them gain from international exchange without becoming excessively vulnerable.

### Q3 Will local cultures disappear because of globalisation?

I don't think they will disappear completely, but some traditions may weaken. Global media gives dominant cultures enormous visibility, especially among younger audiences. If young people rarely encounter local music, stories or customs, their cultural identity may gradually become less distinct. On the other hand, global communication can also introduce local culture to international audiences. The future depends on active preservation. Schools, museums and creators need to keep traditions visible while allowing them to develop rather than freezing them in the past.

## 07 犯罪

**复用逻辑：** 公正执法 → 确定后果 → 威慑；监狱培训 → 就业 → 降低再犯；贫困失业 → 经济型犯罪；过重刑罚 → 改造困难。

### Q1 What is the best way to reduce crime?

There is no single solution, but prevention and consistent enforcement should work together. Fair law enforcement makes the consequences of crime more certain, so potential offenders are more likely to reconsider the risk. At the same time, education and employment support can reduce the economic pressures behind some offences. Extremely harsh punishment alone may overcrowd prisons without addressing the cause. In my view, a balanced system should protect the public, prevent avoidable crime and give suitable offenders a realistic route back into society.

### Q2 Should prisons focus on punishment or rehabilitation?

They need both, but rehabilitation deserves more attention. A prison sentence already restricts a person's freedom, so the period should also prepare them for lawful life afterwards. Education and vocational training can improve employability after release. Stable work then reduces the financial incentive to reoffend. This approach may not suit every violent or high-risk offender, and public safety remains the priority. However, for many prisoners, punishment without skills or support simply returns the same problem to the community later.

### Q3 Why might poverty lead to crime?

Poverty does not make someone criminal, and most poor people obey the law. However, long-term unemployment can limit legal ways of meeting basic needs. Some individuals may then see theft or illegal work as their only practical option. Financial stress can also weaken family stability and community safety. That is why crime prevention should include education, job opportunities and social support as well as policing. These measures do not excuse harmful behaviour, but they can reduce the conditions that make economically motivated crime more likely.

## 08 文化

**复用逻辑：** 节庆与博物馆 → 文化认同 → 传承；跨文化交流 → 创新；强势文化传播 → 同质化；旅游表演化 → 真实性下降。

### Q1 Why should traditional culture be preserved?

Traditional culture helps people understand where their community came from. Festivals, museums and local stories make history visible and strengthen cultural identity. When more people participate, knowledge can pass to the next generation instead of remaining in specialist books. Preservation can also support cultural tourism. However, traditions should not be protected by forcing everyone to follow old customs. The aim is to keep them accessible and meaningful. A living tradition can adapt while retaining the values and skills that make it distinctive.

### Q2 Does contact with other cultures weaken local culture?

It can, but the result is not always negative. If global entertainment receives nearly all the attention, young people may have fewer chances to engage with local traditions. Over time, this can produce cultural homogenisation. On the other hand, cross-cultural exchange exposes artists to new ideas and techniques. They can combine influences and create more diverse forms of expression. So contact itself is not the real threat. Local education, creative confidence and public support determine whether exchange leads to innovation or simple replacement.

### Q3 Can tourism damage traditional culture?

Yes, especially when traditions are redesigned only to satisfy tourists. Ceremonies may be shortened, staged repeatedly or separated from their original meaning. They remain visually attractive, but their authenticity becomes weaker. Tourism can also provide money for heritage conservation and traditional activities, so rejecting visitors completely would be unhelpful. Local communities should control how their culture is presented and receive a fair share of the income. That makes preservation the goal and tourism a supporting tool.

## 09 旅游

**复用逻辑：** 游客消费 → 就业税收；旅游收入 → 文化保护；超出承载力 → 生态受损；短租需求 → 居民迁离。

### Q1 How does tourism benefit local communities?

Tourists spend money on accommodation, food, transport and local activities. This raises revenue for businesses, creates jobs and provides tax income for the area. Cultural attractions may also gain funding for maintenance. For example, entrance fees can support the repair of historic buildings. However, the benefit depends on where the money goes. If most services are owned by outside companies, local residents may gain little. Tourism works best when local businesses participate and part of the revenue improves community facilities.

### Q2 Why are some residents unhappy about tourism?

The main problem is that visitor numbers can exceed a place's capacity. Crowding, waste and heavy resource use may reduce residents' quality of life and damage local ecosystems. Tourism can also increase demand for short-term rentals. As rents rise, some local people may be forced to move, weakening established community networks. Residents often support tourism in principle because it creates work. Their frustration usually comes from unmanaged growth. Visitor limits, housing rules and better infrastructure can make the industry more balanced.

### Q3 Will sustainable tourism become more common?

I think it will, because destinations have a financial reason to protect what attracts visitors. If pollution or overcrowding destroys the local environment, the area's long-term appeal declines. Governments are therefore likely to use booking systems, tourist taxes and stricter waste rules. However, sustainable options can be more expensive, and some businesses will resist short-term limits. Progress may be gradual. The strongest policies will connect environmental protection with local income, so residents and companies benefit from preserving the destination.

## 10 环境

**复用逻辑：** 可再生能源 → 减排；回收设施与教育 → 减少浪费；拥堵费 → 公交与健康；过快转型 → 低收入压力。

### Q1 Who should be responsible for environmental protection?

Responsibility should be shared, but governments and companies have greater power. Governments can invest in renewable energy, improve public transport and enforce pollution rules. Companies can redesign production and packaging, which affects millions of purchases. Individuals still matter because their travel and consumption choices create demand. However, people cannot choose a clean bus if no reliable service exists. The most effective approach combines good infrastructure, clear corporate obligations and practical public education. That makes environmentally responsible behaviour easier rather than relying only on personal guilt.

### Q2 Are congestion charges a good idea?

On balance, yes, especially in crowded city centres. A charge gives drivers a reason to avoid unnecessary car journeys. The revenue can then improve public transport, which further reduces congestion and emissions. Cleaner air also benefits public health. However, the policy can be unfair if low-income workers have no realistic alternative. Cities should introduce reliable buses and exemptions before imposing the fee. With that support, congestion charging can change behaviour while avoiding excessive pressure on vulnerable groups.

### Q3 Why is recycling alone not enough?

Recycling is useful, but it deals with waste after products have already been made and consumed. If people continue buying disposable goods, large amounts of energy and material are still used. Effective waste policy also needs less packaging, durable products and convenient reuse systems. Accessible sorting facilities and public education can improve correct disposal, but companies should reduce unnecessary materials at the source. Overall, recycling is one part of a wider system. It cannot compensate for unlimited consumption on its own.

## 11 健康

**复用逻辑：** 教育筛查 → 早发现 → 降低成本；基本医保 → 缩小健康差距；久坐 → 慢性病；长工时 → 焦虑倦怠。

### Q1 Why is preventive healthcare important?

Preventive healthcare can identify problems before they become severe and expensive. Health education encourages better daily choices, while regular screening allows earlier diagnosis. This reduces complications and can lower long-term pressure on hospitals. For example, detecting high blood pressure early is easier to manage than treating a later emergency. Screening programmes do cost money and should focus on conditions where early action is useful. Overall, prevention protects quality of life and uses healthcare resources more efficiently than waiting for serious illness.

### Q2 Why are health outcomes different across income groups?

Income affects both living conditions and access to treatment. Low-income people may delay seeing a doctor because of cost or inflexible working hours. Preventable illnesses can then become more serious. They may also live in areas with fewer healthy food options or safe places to exercise. Broader basic healthcare coverage can reduce this gap by providing timely treatment. Medical care cannot remove every social difference, but it can stop financial disadvantage from turning a manageable condition into a lasting health crisis.

### Q3 Has modern work harmed people's health?

In some ways, yes. Many urban jobs involve long periods of sitting, which reduces daily energy use and raises the risk of obesity and chronic disease. Long working hours can also cause sleep problems, anxiety and burnout. These effects may damage productivity and family relationships as well as individual health. Modern work is not always worse, since some jobs are safer and remote work can remove commuting. What matters is whether employers set reasonable workloads and whether workers have genuine time to rest and exercise.

## 12 工作

**复用逻辑：** 远程办公 → 平衡与效率；持续培训 → 适应变化；清晰沟通 → 组织韧性；绩效压力 → 倦怠离职。

### Q1 Is remote work better than office work?

It depends on the task and the employee. Remote work removes commuting and gives people more time for rest or family responsibilities. This can improve work-life balance, job satisfaction and productivity. However, less face-to-face contact may create information gaps and slow coordination. New employees may also find it harder to build trust. A hybrid system is often the best compromise. People can complete focused work at home while using office days for discussion, training and tasks that depend on close teamwork.

### Q2 Why should companies train their employees?

Industries change quickly, so existing skills can become outdated. Continuous training helps employees use new tools and respond to changing market needs. This improves their employability and makes the company more competitive. It can also increase loyalty because workers see a path for development. Training is only useful when people can apply it, though. A generic online course completed for administrative reasons achieves very little. Companies should connect learning to real tasks, provide feedback and give employees time to practise.

### Q3 Why do more employees experience burnout?

One important factor is constant performance pressure. Global competition encourages companies to demand greater output, and digital communication keeps some employees available after normal working hours. Sustained overwork can cause poor sleep, anxiety and eventually burnout. Productivity then falls and staff turnover rises, so the pressure becomes costly for the company as well. Individuals can improve boundaries, but managers control workloads and expectations. Clear priorities, realistic deadlines and genuine rest are therefore more effective than simply telling employees to become more resilient.

# 第二部分：6 个口语补充主题

## 13 家庭与代际

**观点来源：** 教育的榜样与同理心、健康的压力链、工作的家庭时间、社会的基本服务公平。

### Q1 Why do parents have a strong influence on children?

Parents shape children's behaviour through repeated daily examples. A child observes how adults manage emotions, treat other people and respond to rules. Positive role models can therefore develop self-discipline and empathy before formal lessons have much effect. Schools later widen this learning by providing teamwork and social responsibility. Parents are not the only influence, especially as children spend more time online. Still, consistent behaviour at home gives children a stable foundation and makes advice more credible than rules that adults do not follow themselves.

### Q2 Are older and younger people under different kinds of pressure?

Yes. Younger people often face academic competition, uncertain employment and high housing costs. Older adults may worry more about health, retirement income and losing independence. The pressures differ, but both groups can suffer when basic services are difficult to access. Younger people may benefit from vocational training and affordable housing, while older people need reliable healthcare and community support. It is therefore unhelpful to argue that one generation has an easier life. Support should reflect different stages of life.

### Q3 Has technology improved family relationships?

It has made contact easier, particularly when relatives live in different cities. Video calls and messaging allow families to share daily events and provide emotional support. However, constant online availability does not guarantee meaningful communication. People may send short messages while avoiding longer conversations, or check their phones during family time. Technology is best treated as a tool rather than a substitute for attention. It strengthens relationships when it supports regular, thoughtful contact, but weakens them when it creates distraction and blurred work-life boundaries.

## 14 城市、住房与交通

**观点来源：** 社会的基本服务、旅游的房租链、环境的拥堵费与污染链、科技的地域公平。

### Q1 Why do many people move to large cities?

Large cities usually offer more jobs, specialised education, healthcare and cultural activities. These services can improve career development and quality of life. Digital industries also tend to concentrate where firms can find skilled workers. However, rapid population growth raises housing demand and can create high rents, congestion and pollution. As a result, the opportunity gap may simply be replaced by a cost-of-living problem. Smaller cities can retain more residents if they provide reliable public services, good transport and diverse employment rather than trying to copy every feature of a capital city.

### Q2 Should governments control housing costs?

They should intervene when housing becomes inaccessible, but simple price controls may create shortages. A better approach combines more housing supply, protection for long-term renters and limits on harmful speculation or excessive short-term rentals. Stable housing allows people to remain near work, schools and community networks. Without it, low-income residents may be pushed far from essential services. Governments must still allow builders a reasonable return. The goal is not to make every home cheap, but to ensure ordinary workers can access safe, stable accommodation.

### Q3 How can cities reduce traffic congestion?

Cities need to make alternatives to driving reliable. Frequent public transport, safe walking routes and connected cycle lanes give people practical choices. Congestion charges can then discourage unnecessary car journeys and provide extra funding for buses or trains. If a fee is introduced before alternatives improve, it mainly punishes people who have no choice. Employers can also support flexible hours or remote work to spread demand. The best strategy changes both infrastructure and incentives instead of simply building more roads for more cars.

## 15 消费、金钱与广告

**观点来源：** 媒体的定向广告链、环境的资源浪费、社会的收入差距、旅游的本地经济链。

### Q1 Why do people buy things they do not need?

People often buy for emotional rather than practical reasons. Advertising links products with status, happiness or belonging, while online platforms repeat personalised offers at exactly the right moment. Convenience also reduces the time available for reflection. As a result, some consumers make impulse purchases beyond their budget and later face financial stress. Buying something enjoyable is not automatically wasteful. The problem is repeated, unaffordable consumption. Waiting before a purchase and disabling targeted notifications can help people separate a real need from a temporary feeling.

### Q2 Does advertising have more influence on young people?

Generally, yes, because younger users spend more time on social platforms and may have less experience recognising commercial persuasion. Targeted advertising can repeatedly connect a product with popularity or success. This makes the message feel personal even when it is automated. Adults are also influenced, especially when advertising exploits health or financial concerns. Children deserve stronger protection because they cannot evaluate these techniques equally. Schools can build media literacy, while platforms should restrict behavioural advertising aimed at minors.

### Q3 Is consumer spending good for society?

It supports businesses, employment and tax revenue, so it is essential to the economy. Spending at local shops can circulate income within a community. However, unlimited consumption increases packaging, resource use and waste. It can also deepen social pressure if people judge success mainly through possessions. On balance, the issue is not spending itself but what people buy and whether they can afford it. Durable goods, repair services and local experiences can support economic activity with lower environmental and financial costs.

## 16 体育与休闲

**观点来源：** 健康的运动与预防链、工作的休息与压力链、社会凝聚力、政府公共服务投入。

### Q1 Why do many adults exercise less than they should?

The main barriers are time, routine and access. Long working hours leave people tired, while sedentary jobs make inactivity feel normal. Some neighbourhoods also lack affordable facilities or safe outdoor space. As energy expenditure falls, the risks of obesity and chronic disease rise. Telling people to be disciplined is not enough. Employers can protect reasonable working hours, and local governments can provide parks and sports facilities. When exercise is convenient and social, it is much easier to maintain as a habit.

### Q2 Should governments spend money on public sports facilities?

Yes, if the facilities are accessible and match local needs. Parks, courts and swimming pools make exercise affordable, especially for families who cannot pay for private clubs. Regular activity supports preventive healthcare and can reduce long-term medical costs. Shared facilities may also strengthen community connections. However, impressive stadiums with little public use are a poor investment. Governments should prioritise simple neighbourhood facilities, maintain them properly and measure participation rather than treating construction itself as success.

### Q3 Is leisure time becoming more important?

I think so, because many people face constant performance pressure and digital availability. Genuine leisure allows physical recovery and reduces anxiety, which can prevent burnout. It can also protect family relationships because people have time to communicate without work interruptions. However, passive screen use does not always provide the same recovery as exercise, hobbies or social contact. In the future, people may value leisure more, but employers must respect boundaries. Otherwise, free time will continue to be interrupted by work messages.

## 17 人际关系、合作与领导

**观点来源：** 工作的清晰沟通与团队凝聚力、教育的同理心、国际与文化交流、媒体的信任链。

### Q1 What makes someone a good leader?

A good leader communicates decisions and responsibilities clearly. When people understand the direction and their own role, uncertainty falls and cooperation becomes more stable. Good leaders also listen because team members may notice risks that managers cannot see. For example, realistic feedback from employees can prevent an impossible deadline from causing burnout. Confidence matters, but leadership is not simply giving orders. It is creating trust, making informed decisions and adjusting the plan when evidence shows that the original approach is weak.

### Q2 Why is teamwork difficult for some people?

Teamwork requires people to share information, accept different approaches and depend on others. Some find this uncomfortable because they want full control or have experienced unreliable teammates. Remote communication can add information gaps and coordination delays. Clear responsibilities and regular updates reduce these problems. Schools can also help by using group projects with individual accountability. Teamwork does not mean everyone agrees all the time. It means disagreement is handled constructively and the group remains focused on a shared outcome.

### Q3 Has online communication made people less trusting?

In some situations, yes. Online messages lack tone and body language, so misunderstandings are easier. False information, anonymous accounts and privacy violations can also reduce trust in digital interaction. However, online tools help people maintain relationships across distance and allow teams to share information quickly. The effect depends on how they are used. Clear communication, reliable identity checks and respect for personal data can build confidence. Trust becomes weaker when speed and engagement are valued more than accuracy and accountability.

## 18 成功、竞争与规则

**观点来源：** 教育竞争与考试链、工作的绩效压力、犯罪的公平执法、政府的适度监管。

### Q1 Is competition good for children?

Moderate competition can motivate children and help them measure progress. It may encourage practice, persistence and higher standards. However, intense academic competition can turn learning into a constant search for scores. Practical exploration is then pushed aside, while stress and family spending on tutoring increase. Children may even see classmates only as rivals. Competition is healthiest when improvement matters more than ranking and when failure provides feedback. Schools should therefore combine individual goals, cooperative tasks and fair assessment.

### Q2 Do successful people always work harder than others?

Hard work matters, but it is not the whole explanation. People also begin with different educational resources, social networks and financial security. Someone with good training can enter the labour market with more relevant skills, while another person may spend the same effort overcoming basic barriers. Luck and timing also affect opportunities. Recognising these differences does not dismiss individual responsibility. It simply gives a more accurate picture. Society should reward effort while ensuring that access to education and essential services is not determined entirely by family background.

### Q3 Why are fair rules important?

Fair rules make consequences predictable. In schools, workplaces and society, people are more willing to cooperate when standards apply consistently. In law enforcement, certainty and fairness can deter harmful behaviour more effectively than extreme but irregular punishment. Rules also protect weaker people from arbitrary decisions. However, too many rules create administrative costs and resistance. A good rule should address a clear problem, be explained openly and be reviewed when circumstances change. Fairness depends on both the content of a rule and the way it is enforced.
