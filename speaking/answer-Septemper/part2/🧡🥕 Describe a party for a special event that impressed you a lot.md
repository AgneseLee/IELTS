## Cue Card

**🧡 Describe a party for a special event that impressed you a lot**

> **You should say:**
> - When and where it was held
> - What the party was held for
> - How many people attended it
> - And explain why it impressed you a lot

> Bank: B4 | Modules: layoff / advice / catch-up

## 1. Band 7 Answer

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
It was about three months ago. My company went through a round of layoffs, and I received a much heavier workload afterwards. I had trouble falling asleep. I just couldn't fall asleep naturally. I lay in bed and couldn't stop thinking about my future, my career, and everything that was bothering me. Anyway, that's why I said yes when my best friend Stella invited me to a catch-up last May at her apartment in Beijing. <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">There were about three of us, all close friends.</mark> We just lay on the sofa, had some wine, and talked about books like Harry Potter and The Great Gatsby. Our conversations ranged from literature to movies and music, but never about money, cars, or houses. Eventually, our conversation drifted to my concerns about work. Stella listened carefully and said something that has stayed with me: <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">"Never treat exhaustion as an achievement."</mark>
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
That saying really made me rethink my understanding of resilience and gradually changed my mindset. I used to believe that strong people should solve every problem on their own and keep working even under huge pressure. Now I realize that treating exhaustion as an achievement was actually <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">a mistaken way of thinking</mark>. Real strength involves asking for help, accepting support, and changing direction when things don't go as planned. Since then, I've learned how to ask for help from my friends or colleagues instead of pushing myself so hard. <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">That's why this gathering impressed me so much. It wasn't just a catch-up—it completely changed the way I think about resilience.</mark>
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->

## 2. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: I openly shared my concerns with Stella instead of pretending everything was fine.
- Secondary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: I reconsidered my belief that strength meant handling everything alone and became more willing to accept support.

## 3. Story Bank

- Who/What: A catch-up with Stella and another close friend at her apartment in Beijing.
- Background: A round of layoffs about three months ago left me with a heavier workload and trouble sleeping. Stella invited me to a catch-up last May.
- Main event: We lay on the sofa, had wine, and talked about literature, movies, and music before discussing my work concerns. Stella said, “Never treat exhaustion as an achievement.”
- Ability shown: Sharing my concerns honestly and becoming more willing to accept support.
- Reflection: The gathering changed my understanding of resilience: real strength includes asking for help, accepting support, and changing direction.
