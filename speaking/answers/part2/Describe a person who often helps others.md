## Cue Card

**Describe a person who often helps others**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - How often he/she helps others
> - How/why he/she helps others
> - And how you feel about this person

> Bank: B4 | Modules: new-colleague / setup-notes / cooperative-culture

## 1. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Communication Skills
- Reason: Stella listens to newcomers, explains complex systems clearly, and makes it safe for them to ask questions.
- Secondary Ability: Leadership & Collaboration
- Reason: She shares reusable notes and encourages colleagues to support the next newcomer.

## 2. Story Bank

- Who/What: Stella, my former colleague, who quietly supports new team members.
- Background: Our software systems were complicated and often confused newcomers.
- Main event: She helped me set up my environment and regularly guided other colleagues through the same process.
- Ability shown: Clear explanation and practical support that strengthen the whole team.
- Reflection: Her help taught me that kindness can become a cooperative working culture.

## 3. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to describe is Stella, my former colleague. She helps people almost every week, especially newcomers who are unfamiliar with our software systems. When I joined her project, the setup process was confusing and the official guide was outdated. Stella shared her own notes and spent an afternoon explaining each stage in plain language. She stayed available when I had questions and never made me feel incompetent. Later, I saw her give the same patient support to other colleagues. She helps because she remembers how stressful a new workplace can feel.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I feel grateful and impressed. Her help saved me several days, but more importantly, it made me feel understood and supported during a difficult moment.

She communicates effectively because she listens first and breaks complicated work into manageable steps. Her notes also created a system others could reuse. When another colleague joined, I followed Stella's example and guided him through the setup. Then I realised her kindness had spread beyond one person and strengthened our shared responsibility. She changed my view of helping. It does not require a dramatic sacrifice. Clear guidance, patience, and a little time can build trust and make a workplace more cooperative. I now offer help before confusion becomes embarrassment.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
