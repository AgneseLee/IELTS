## Final Integrated Version (7.0-7.5)
**Word count: 336**

The debate surrounding an ageing population has engaged a broad audience recently. Although having more elderly people may bring some social benefits, its negative effects are more serious and long-lasting. Therefore, I believe that the disadvantages of this trend outweigh its advantages.

It is important to recognize that an ageing population can produce harmful effects on the labour market. A central problem is that it increases pressure on working-age adults, who may have to earn more money in order to support elderly people by paying higher taxes than before. Without sufficient tax income, it would be difficult to fund the pension and elderly care systems properly. In addition, due to a lack of sufficient young workers, companies may struggle to operate efficiently and effectively, and countries' tax revenue may decline accordingly.

Another significant drawback of this trend is its tendency to cause resource misallocation by governments. The main reason is that it forces governments to divert limited money away from other public services, especially towards healthcare and pensions, thereby reducing investment in education and other welfare services. On the one hand, it places an additional burden on the medical system, which has to deal with a large number of elderly patients. On the other hand, education for young people may deteriorate due to a lack of funding. As a result, this trend often creates systemic inefficiencies rather than fulfilling its intended goals.

Admittedly, elderly people can still make useful contributions to society. For example, many older citizens have valuable work experience and can provide practical advice to younger generations. Some of them may also help their families by caring for grandchildren, which allows young parents to focus more on their careers. Nevertheless, these benefits are limited, because not all elderly people are healthy enough to keep working or provide regular support for their families.

In conclusion, although an ageing population may bring certain benefits, I believe it is mainly a negative trend because it places heavy pressure on the workforce, public finances and essential public services.

## New Shorter Version (7.0)
**Word count: 283**

The debate surrounding an ageing population has engaged a broad audience recently. Although having more elderly people may bring some social benefits, I believe that its disadvantages are more serious than its advantages.

It is important to recognize that an ageing population can produce harmful effects on the labour market. A central problem is that it increases pressure on working-age adults, who may have to pay higher taxes to support elderly people. Without sufficient tax income, it would be difficult for governments to fund pension and elderly care systems properly. In addition, due to a lack of young workers, companies may struggle to operate efficiently, and national tax revenue may decline accordingly.

Another significant drawback of this trend is its tendency to cause resource misallocation by governments. The main reason is that it forces governments to divert limited money away from other public services, especially towards healthcare and pensions. On the one hand, it places an additional burden on the medical system, which has to deal with a large number of elderly patients. On the other hand, education for young people may deteriorate due to a lack of funding. As a result, this trend often creates systemic inefficiencies rather than improving social welfare.

Admittedly, elderly people can still contribute to society by offering experience, advice and family support. For example, some retired people help care for their grandchildren, which can reduce pressure on young parents. Nevertheless, this advantage is limited because many older people also need medical care and financial support themselves.

In conclusion, although an ageing population may bring certain benefits, I believe it is mainly a negative trend because it places heavy pressure on the workforce, public finances and public services.
