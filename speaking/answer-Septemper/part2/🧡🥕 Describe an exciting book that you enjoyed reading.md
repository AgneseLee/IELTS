> Bank: B2 | Modules: Counselling-for-Toads / sleep-pressure / self-reflection

## 1. Band 7 Answer (1:40–2:00)

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
About three months ago, I had trouble falling asleep. I lay in bed worrying about my career and future. I picked up *Counselling for Toads*, a book on my shelf that I'd bought years ago but had never read. It's a psychology book <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">told through</mark> animal characters. It follows Toad's conversations with Heron, a counsellor who helps him understand his feelings. <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">I found it exciting because I recognised myself in Toad.</mark> Each conversation made me curious about what he'd discover next, especially when his reactions felt like my own.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
My main takeaway was "slow is fast." I used to think I had to handle every challenge alone and finish everything immediately. I thought that would prove I was strong and capable. But that pressure left me exhausted and made it harder to think clearly.

After reading, I started writing down my feelings instead of pushing them aside. I realised that slowing down didn't mean falling behind. It helped me focus on what I could control and what mattered most at that moment. Now, slowing down means taking time to understand what's wrong before acting. That change in perspective is why the book stayed with me.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->
