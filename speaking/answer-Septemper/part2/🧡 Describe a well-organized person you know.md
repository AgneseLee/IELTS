## Cue Card

**🧡 Describe a well-organized person you know**

> **You should say:**
> - Who this person is
> - What this person usually does to stay organized
> - In what situations you have noticed this quality
> - And explain why you think being organized is important to him/her

> Bank: B4 | Modules: priorities / manageable-steps / boundaries

## 1. Band 7 Answer

<!-- NARRATIVE_START -->
The person I'd like to talk about is Stella, my former colleague and one of my best friends. About three months ago, our company went through a layoff, and that's when I realized that she was a really well-organized person. After the layoff, Stella received a much heavier workload. She began sleeping badly at night and even started showing signs of serious burnout. To stay organized and protect her health, she wrote down her main priorities each morning and broke larger tasks into smaller steps she could handle. She also left some time for unexpected work instead of filling every minute of the day. With this well-organized schedule, she gradually recovered both physically and mentally within a few weeks.
<!-- NARRATIVE_END -->

<!-- REFLECTION_START -->
I think being organized is important to her because it helps her manage a demanding job while protecting her health. Having clear priorities means she knows what to focus on at the moment and what can wait, so she feels more in control of her work. Looking back, her experience changed my view of being organized. I used to think it simply meant fitting more tasks into a day, but now I think it includes setting realistic goals, accepting support, and changing plans when things don't go as expected. I admire her because her recovery didn't happen overnight, but she kept working to improve her situation. If I face career challenges in the future, I'll follow her method: stay calm and take one manageable step at a time. Most importantly, I'll never treat exhaustion as an achievement.
<!-- REFLECTION_END -->

## 2. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Problem-solving Skills
- Reason: Stella identifies urgent tasks, sets daily priorities, and breaks larger tasks into manageable steps.
- Secondary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: She adjusted her schedule when her increased workload affected her health.

- Who/What: Stella, my former colleague and one of my best friends.
- Daily habits: She writes down priorities, breaks larger tasks into smaller steps, and leaves time for unexpected work.
- Background: Company layoffs left her with a heavier workload, poor sleep, and a sense of being overwhelmed.
- Main event: After the layoff, I noticed how she wrote down priorities, divided larger tasks, and left time for unexpected work to protect her health.
- Ability shown: Practical planning and willingness to adapt an unhealthy routine.
- Reflection: Being organized helps her manage demanding work while protecting her health; it means making realistic plans rather than filling every minute.
