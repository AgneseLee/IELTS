## Cue Card

**❤️ Describe an advertisement with a famous person in it**

> **You should say:**
> - Who the person is
> - Where you can see it
> - What the advertisement is about
> - And explain how you feel about the advertisement

> Bank: B5 | Modules: badminton-player / training-story / sleep-pressure

## 1. Band 7 Answer

About three months ago, I had trouble falling asleep. I just couldn't fall asleep naturally. I literally lay in bed but couldn't stop thinking about my future and my career. I was basically worried about everything that was bothering me. One night, I couldn't sleep again, so I turned on the TV, and that was when I first saw an advertisement featuring a famous Chinese badminton player, Sun Yang. It was an advertisement for sports shoes. Honestly, I can hardly remember most of the content. I just remember some scenes. The badminton player was training early in the morning. He missed several shots, but he didn't give up and kept practicing consistently. There was a slogan saying, “Slow is fast.” The advertisement ended, but the slogan left a lasting impression on me. I felt the ad was quite credible because the player is famous for his achievements. I also appreciated the ad because it conveyed the idea that success isn't just about talent or sudden luck. It's more about consistent effort and mindset. I used to think I was the kind of person who had to finish every task immediately, handle every challenge alone, and fix every problem immediately to prove that I was capable and strong enough. But that really put a lot of pressure on me and made me feel exhausted and nervous. When I saw the slogan in the advertisement, I felt a little relieved, as if some of my stress had been lifted. I realized that slowing down didn't mean falling behind. Instead, it allowed me to focus on what I could control and what mattered most at that moment.

## 2. Core Ability Mapping

- Primary Ability: Adaptability
- Reason: I reconsidered my habit of rushing and focused on what I could control after seeing the slogan.
